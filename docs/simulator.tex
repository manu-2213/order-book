\begin{abstract}
This document is the self-contained technical manual and research record for the study
\emph{Hiding in the Order Flow: Learning to Execute Against Liquidity Providers that Detect Metaorders}
and its accompanying Python package \code{order\_book}. It is written for two audiences at once:
(i)~computer scientists, machine-learning researchers, and software engineers who may have \emph{no
prior background in market microstructure or quantitative finance}, and (ii)~market-microstructure
researchers who require exact mathematical equations, algorithmic pseudocode, data-structure
invariants, and empirical tables ready to be lifted into a paper's methodology and results sections.

We begin with a pedagogical primer on limit order books, metaorders, implementation shortfall,
mechanical market impact, and information leakage (\S\ref{sec:primer}). We then map the entire
codebase file by file, explaining what data structures each module uses, why those structures were
chosen, and how data and control flow through a single simulation step (\S\ref{sec:codebase}). Next we
specify the matching engine (\code{OrderBook}), accounting and routing layer (\code{Market}), rolling
public state (\code{PublicState}), and tree-structured random-number architecture that guarantees
exact byte-for-byte replay and common-random-number counterfactuals (\S\S\ref{sec:book}--\ref{sec:rng}).
We derive the exact mathematical update equations and decision rules of every background agent
(\code{FundamentalValue}, \code{NoiseFlow}, \code{Speculators}, \code{InstitutionalTrader},
\code{MarketMaker}, \code{OrderAnticipator}) and every metaorder detector (the rule-based A1
\code{FlowDetector}, the four held-out A2 statistical detectors, and the learned A3 multinomial
logistic classifier). We then document the twelve classical, model-based, and stealth-heuristic
execution baselines, the A0 calibration protocol, the rank-based and paired-bootstrap statistical
methodology, and the full results of all validation and Phase~2 measurement experiments (E1, E2, E3,
E7, and E7b). Finally, Part~\ref{part:three} documents the Phase~3 reinforcement-learning execution
environment (\code{ExecutionEnv}), the 13-dimensional sign-normalised observation encoder, the 15-way
action space, the telescoping implementation-shortfall reward theorem, the Proximal Policy
Optimisation (\code{PPOTrainer}) implementation, the parallel Weights \& Biases hyperparameter sweep
(\code{sweep\_rl.py}), and the empirical results showing the learned policy outperforming TWAP.
\end{abstract}

\section{A Self-Contained Primer for Non-Finance Readers}
\label{sec:primer}
%==================================================================================================

If your background is in reinforcement learning, multi-agent systems, or computer science rather than
finance, this section defines every domain concept used in the project from first principles.

\subsection{What is a Limit Order Book (LOB)?}

Modern electronic financial exchanges (such as NASDAQ, NYSE, or Binance) do not have a central dealer
who sets a single price. Instead, they run a deterministic data structure called a \textbf{Limit Order
Book (LOB)} that matches buyers and sellers directly.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Ticks and lots.} To avoid floating-point rounding ambiguities, prices and quantities are
  discrete integers. The smallest price increment is one \textbf{tick} (for example, \$0.01). In our
  simulator, the initial price is $10\,000$ ticks (which you can think of as \$100.00). The smallest
  quantity unit is one \textbf{lot} (or ``share''). Cash is measured in $\text{ticks}\times\text{lots}$.
\item \textbf{Sides (\code{"bid"} vs.\ \code{"ask"}).} A \textbf{bid} is an order to \emph{buy}. An
  \textbf{ask} (or offer) is an order to \emph{sell}.
\item \textbf{Limit orders vs.\ market orders.}
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item A \textbf{limit order} specifies a side, a quantity $q$, and a worst acceptable price $p$. If a
    buyer submits a limit bid at $p = 9\,999$ while the cheapest seller is asking $10\,001$, no trade
    can occur immediately; the bid \emph{rests} in the book's bid queue at price $9\,999$, waiting for a
    seller. Resting limit orders \emph{supply liquidity} (the trader is acting as a \textbf{maker}).
  \item A \textbf{market order} specifies only a side and a quantity $q$: ``buy $q$ lots right now at
    whatever prices are currently resting on the opposite side of the book.'' Market orders (and limit
    orders priced aggressively enough to cross the opposite side immediately) \emph{consume liquidity}
    (the trader is acting as an \textbf{aggressor} or \textbf{taker}).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Best bid, best ask, mid-price, and bid--ask spread.} At any instant, the highest resting
  buy price is the \textbf{best bid} $b_t$, and the lowest resting sell price is the \textbf{best ask}
  $a_t$. Because any bid with $p \ge a_t$ would have matched immediately upon arrival, a resting book is
  \emph{never crossed}: $b_t < a_t$ always. From $b_t$ and $a_t$ we define two fundamental quantities:
  \begin{equation}
    \text{Mid-price:}\quad m_t = \frac{b_t + a_t}{2}, \qquad
    \text{Spread:}\quad s_t = a_t - b_t \ge 1\text{ tick}.
  \end{equation}
  If you buy 1 lot immediately with a market order, you pay $a_t = m_t + s_t/2$; if you sell 1 lot
  immediately, you receive $b_t = m_t - s_t/2$. Thus crossing the spread costs $s_t/2$ ticks per lot
  relative to the mid-price even before moving the price levels.
\item \textbf{Price--time priority (FIFO).} When an incoming buy order matches against resting asks,
  which ask gets filled first? First, the lowest-priced ask (\emph{price priority}). Second, among
  multiple asks resting at that same lowest price, the one that arrived earliest (\emph{time priority},
  or First-In-First-Out, FIFO). Consequently, keeping your place near the front of the queue at a price
  level (\textbf{queue position}) is valuable.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is Optimal Execution and a Metaorder?}

Suppose an institutional investor (a pension fund or mutual fund) decides to buy $Q = 2\,000$ lots of
an asset, when typical resting volume at the best ask is only $15$ lots and the whole market trades
only $\bar V \approx 3$--$5$ lots per second.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item This large parent intention of size $Q$ is called a \textbf{metaorder}.
\item If the investor dumps all $Q = 2\,000$ lots into the book as a single market order at $t=0$
  (the \code{immediate} strategy), the order will wipe out every resting ask level from $a_0$ all the
  way up the book, paying terrible prices.
\item Instead, an \textbf{execution algorithm} (the \textbf{executor}) splits the parent order $Q$ into
  many small \textbf{child orders} over a time horizon $T$ (say $T = 2\,000$ steps, making a decision
  every $N = 5$ steps).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How Do We Measure Execution Cost? (Implementation Shortfall)}

Let $m_{\text{arrival}} = m_{t_0}$ be the mid-price at the moment $t_0$ when the executor starts its
metaorder. Over the horizon, the executor obtains a sequence of child fills $(p_k, q_k)$ with
$\sum_k q_k = Q$, achieving a quantity-weighted average fill price
\begin{equation}
  \bar p = \frac{1}{Q}\sum_{k} p_k q_k = \frac{\text{Total Notional Paid/Received}}{Q}.
\end{equation}
The standard industry metric of execution cost is \textbf{Implementation Shortfall (IS)}, expressed in
\textbf{basis points (bps)}, where $1\text{ bp} = 10^{-4} = 0.01\%$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:is_def}
  \text{IS (bps)} = \sign \cdot \frac{\bar p - m_{\text{arrival}}}{m_{\text{arrival}}} \times 10^4,
  \qquad \sign = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{if buying (\code{"bid"})}, \\ -1 & \text{if selling (\code{"ask"})}. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
A \emph{positive} IS means the executor paid more than $m_{\text{arrival}}$ when buying (or received
less than $m_{\text{arrival}}$ when selling) --- i.e.\ \textbf{positive IS is a cost}. For example, at
$m_{\text{arrival}} = 10\,000$ ticks, $1\text{ tick}$ of adverse price movement equals exactly
$1\text{ bp}$ of implementation shortfall.

\subsection{Mechanical Market Impact vs.\ Information Leakage (The Core Distinction)}

Why is $\text{IS} > 0$ when executing a large metaorder? There are two distinct mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Mechanical market impact.} Every child buy order physically removes resting sell orders
  from the book and shifts market makers' inventories (they become short shares, so they shift their
  quotes upward to mean-revert their inventory). Even if no agent in the market suspects that a large
  metaorder is underway, consuming $Q$ lots of liquidity mechanically pushes the price up. Across
  real equity markets, mechanical peak impact follows the famous empirical \textbf{square-root law}:
  $\Delta m \propto \sigma\sqrt{Q/V}$.
\item \textbf{Information leakage (detection cost).} Because a metaorder splits $Q$ into dozens of
  same-direction child orders over time, it leaves a statistical \emph{footprint} in the public order
  flow (persistent buy imbalance, long runs of buy trades, regular child sizes). Sophisticated
  liquidity providers (\textbf{detecting market makers}) and predatory traders (\textbf{order
  anticipators}) monitor public order flow to infer whether a metaorder is active. When their
  \textbf{detector} fires ($s_t > 0$):
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Detecting market makers \emph{skew} their quotes upward, \emph{widen} their ask spread, and
    \emph{cut} their ask quote size to protect themselves from being adversely selected;
  \item Order anticipators \emph{front-run} the metaorder by buying ahead of the executor and selling
    back into the elevated price.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

In real markets, you can never observe what a metaorder \emph{would} have cost if detectors had not
spotted it. In our deterministic simulator, we can measure this \textbf{exact counterfactual}: we run
the exact same random seed twice --- once where the executor's trades are \textbf{visible} to the public
order-flow statistics and detectors ($\text{IS}_{\text{visible}}$), and once where the executor's trades
still execute and move prices and inventories identically in the book, but are \textbf{hidden} from the
public flow window ($\text{IS}_{\text{invisible}}$, via \code{Market.hide(cid)}). This yields the clean
additive decomposition:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:leakage_decomp}
  \underbrace{\text{IS}_{\text{visible}}}_{\text{Total Execution Cost}}
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\text{IS}_{\text{invisible}}}_{\text{Mechanical Impact Cost}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\big(\text{IS}_{\text{visible}} - \text{IS}_{\text{invisible}}\big)}_{\text{Information Leakage Cost}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Strict Downward Import Hierarchy}

A core architectural invariant of the codebase is that dependencies point strictly downward:
\begin{align*}
  \code{book}
  \;\longleftarrow\;
  \code{market},\, \code{public\_state}
  \;\longleftarrow\;
  \code{detectors}
  \;\longleftarrow\;
  \code{agents}
  \;\longleftarrow\;
  \code{simulator} \\
  \;\longleftarrow\;
  \code{presets},\, \code{execution},\, \code{experiments}
  \;\longleftarrow\;
  \code{scripts}.
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \code{OrderBook} (\code{book.py}) imports \emph{nothing} from \code{order\_book}; it knows only
  integer client IDs, order IDs, prices, and quantities.
\item \code{Detector} subclasses (\code{detectors/}) import only \code{PublicState}; they have no
  reference to \code{OrderBook}, \code{Market}, or any agent, making it structurally impossible for a
  detector to cheat by inspecting order IDs or client accounts.
\item Background agents and \code{ExecutorAgent} hold a reference to \code{Market}, never mutating
  \code{OrderBook} directly, so cash and share reservations in \code{Market} can never go out of sync
  with resting orders in \code{OrderBook}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Anatomy of a Single Simulation Step (\code{Simulator.step()})}

Every call to \code{Simulator.step()} in \code{order\_book/simulator.py} executes the following
six-stage pipeline in exact deterministic order:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
def step(self) -> None:
    self.fundamental.advance()                     # 1. Advance hidden fundamental value v_t
    for agent in self.externals:                   # 2. External agents (ExecutorAgent) act first
        agent.act()
    agents = self.agents
    for i in self.rng.permutation(len(agents)):    # 3. Background agents act in random order
        agents[i].act()
    self.market.end_step()                         # 4. Refresh PublicState & clear step accumulators
    public = self.market.public
    for detector in self.detectors.values():       # 5. Update every registered detector from PublicState
        detector.update(public)
\end{lstlisting}
Notice two crucial subtleties of this loop:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Why \code{externals} act outside \code{self.rng.permutation(len(agents))}.} If adding an
  external agent (such as \code{ExecutorAgent} or the paired-impact injected trader) changed the length
  of \code{self.agents}, then \code{self.rng.permutation} would consume a different number of random
  bytes on step~1, immediately scrambling the action order of all background agents relative to a
  no-executor run. Keeping \code{externals} in a separate list that acts before the permutation preserves
  common random numbers across paired counterfactual runs (\S\ref{sec:rng}).
\item \textbf{When \code{PublicState.step\_volume} and \code{detectors} update.} During stages 2 and 3,
  every trade that settles in \code{Market.\_settle()} accumulates into \code{Market.\_buy\_volume},
  \code{\_sell\_volume}, \code{\_flow\_buy}, \code{\_flow\_sell}, and \code{\_step\_trades}. At stage~4,
  \code{Market.end\_step()} pushes those step totals into \code{PublicState.update(...)}, increments
  \code{Market.step} from $t$ to $t+1$, and zeroes the accumulators. Immediately after, at stage~5, all
  detectors read the newly updated \code{PublicState}. Thus, on step $t+1$, when agents act in stages 2
  and 3, they observe the \code{PublicState} and detector signals resulting from step $t$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Matching Engine and Data Structures (\code{order\_book.book})}
\label{sec:book}
%==================================================================================================

\code{OrderBook} (\code{order\_book/book.py}) is a single-instrument, price--time (FIFO) limit order
book. Because a single simulation episode executes tens of thousands of order placements, cancellations,
and queries, every hot-path operation is engineered to avoid linear scans over resting orders.

\subsection{Internal Data Structures and Complexity Guarantees}

\code{OrderBook} maintains five synchronised internal containers:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Per-price FIFO queues:}
  \code{\_queues: dict[Side, dict[int, OrderedDict[int, \_Order]]]}. For each side (\code{"bid"} or
  \code{"ask"}) and each active price $p$, \code{\_queues[side][p]} is a Python \code{OrderedDict}
  mapping \code{order\_id} $\mapsto$ \code{\_Order} (a lightweight \code{\_\_slots\_\_} object holding
  \code{order\_id, client\_id, side, price, quantity}). Because \code{OrderedDict} is a doubly-linked
  hash table in CPython:
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Appending a new resting order at the back of the FIFO queue is $O(1)$;
  \item Inspecting the oldest resting order at the front of the queue (\code{next(iter(queue.values()))})
    is $O(1)$;
  \item Deleting \emph{any} order by \code{order\_id} from the middle of the queue upon cancellation is
    also $O(1)$!
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Incremental per-level volume cache:}
  \code{\_volumes: dict[Side, dict[int, int]]}. Stores the sum of resting quantities at price $p$,
  updated incrementally in \code{\_add}, \code{\_remove}, and partial fills in \code{\_match}. Querying
  \code{get\_volume\_at\_price(side, p)} is $O(1)$ without summing over orders.
\item \textbf{Sorted active price lists:}
  \code{\_prices: dict[Side, list[int]]}. Maintained in ascending sorted order via \code{bisect.insort}
  and \code{bisect\_left} when a price level is created or emptied. Because the bounded window allows at
  most $N_{\text{levels}} = 20$ active prices per side, \code{\_prices[side]} has length $\le 20$. The
  best bid is \code{\_prices["bid"][-1]} ($O(1)$) and the best ask is \code{\_prices["ask"][0]} ($O(1)$).
\item \textbf{Global order index:}
  \code{\_orders: dict[int, \_Order]} maps every live \code{order\_id} $\mapsto$ \code{\_Order} in $O(1)$.
\item \textbf{Per-client order index:}
  \code{\_client\_orders: dict[int, dict[int, None]]} maps each \code{client\_id} to an insertion-ordered
  set of its currently resting \code{order\_id}s, making \code{get\_client\_orders(cid)} and
  \code{cancel\_all(cid)} $O(k_{\text{client}})$ in the client's own resting orders rather than
  $O(N_{\text{book}})$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Bounded Price Window and Automatic Eviction}

To keep observation arrays fixed-dimensional and prevent abandoned far-away orders from accumulating
forever, each side only admits limit prices within $N_{\text{levels}} = 20$ ticks (\code{num\_levels})
of its \textbf{anchor} $\mathcal{A}(\text{side})$:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{A}(\text{bid}) &= \begin{cases}
    b_t & \text{if bid side non-empty}, \\
    a_t - 1 & \text{if bid empty, ask non-empty}, \\
    p_{\text{last}} \text{ (or } m_0 - d_0\text{)} & \text{if both sides empty},
  \end{cases} \\
  \mathcal{A}(\text{ask}) &= \begin{cases}
    a_t & \text{if ask side non-empty}, \\
    b_t + 1 & \text{if ask empty, bid non-empty}, \\
    p_{\text{last}} \text{ (or } m_0 + d_0\text{)} & \text{if both sides empty}.
  \end{cases}
\end{align}
The active window for bids is $[\max(1, \mathcal{A}(\text{bid}) - N_{\text{levels}} + 1),\; +\infty)$
and for asks is $[1,\; \mathcal{A}(\text{ask}) + N_{\text{levels}} - 1]$. Whenever an aggressive fill or
a price-improving limit order shifts $\mathcal{A}(\text{side})$, \code{OrderBook.\_refresh(side)} pops
all resting orders that now lie outside the new window edge and returns them in
\code{report["evicted\_orders"]} so \code{Market} can immediately release their cash/share reservations.

\subsection{Matching, Self-Trade Prevention (STP), and Order Modification}

When an order $(c, \text{side}, q, p_{\text{limit}})$ enters \code{OrderBook.\_match}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item While the opposite side has resting prices $p_{\text{rest}}$ that cross $p_{\text{limit}}$
  ($p_{\text{rest}} \le p_{\text{limit}}$ for a buy, $p_{\text{rest}} \ge p_{\text{limit}}$ for a sell,
  or unconditionally for a market order where $p_{\text{limit}} = \text{None}$) and $q > 0$:
\item Inspect the oldest resting order $o_{\text{rest}}$ at $p_{\text{rest}}$.
\item \textbf{Self-Trade Prevention (STP):} If $o_{\text{rest}}.\text{client\_id} == c$ and
  $\text{mode} \neq \code{"none"}$:
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \code{"cancel\_newest"} (default): cancel the incoming order's remaining quantity $q \leftarrow 0$
    and stop matching;
  \item \code{"cancel\_oldest"}: remove $o_{\text{rest}}$ from the book (recording it in
    \code{stp\_cancelled\_orders}) and continue matching $q$;
  \item \code{"cancel\_both"}: remove $o_{\text{rest}}$ \emph{and} cancel the incoming remainder $q \leftarrow 0$.
  \end{itemize}
\item Otherwise, execute a trade of size $\Delta q = \min(q, o_{\text{rest}}.\text{quantity})$ at the
  \textbf{resting order's price} $p_{\text{rest}}$. Decrement both quantities; if $o_{\text{rest}}$ is
  exhausted, call \code{\_remove($o_{\text{rest}}$)}.
\item After the loop, if the order is a limit order and $q > 0$ remains, insert the remainder into the
  resting book via \code{\_add}. If it is a market order, any unfilled remainder is cancelled
  (immediate-or-cancel semantics).
\item \textbf{Order modification (\code{modify\_order}):} If a client modifies an existing resting order
  by \emph{reducing} its quantity at the \emph{same} price, its \code{quantity} is decremented in place
  and it retains its FIFO queue position. If the quantity is increased or the price is changed, the old
  order is atomically removed and replaced with a new \code{order\_id} at the back of the target queue.
\end{itemize}

%==================================================================================================
\section[Accounting, Reservations, and Routing]{Accounting, Reservations, and Routing (\code{order\_book.market})}
\label{sec:market}
%==================================================================================================

\code{Market} (\code{order\_book/market.py}) sits between all agents and \code{OrderBook}. Every agent
has a unique integer client ID $\text{cid}$ and an \code{Account} with four integer fields:
\[
  (\text{cash}_i,\; \text{shares}_i,\; \text{reserved\_cash}_i,\; \text{reserved\_shares}_i).
\]
No short-selling and no borrowing are permitted.

\subsection{Exact Reservation Bookkeeping}

To prevent an agent from placing ten resting limit bids that collectively exceed its cash if all ten
were filled, \code{Market} reserves funds when an order rests and releases them when it leaves:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Placement cap:} When client $i$ submits \code{Market.limit(cid, side, p, q)}, its
  quantity is first capped by what its \emph{unreserved} balance can fund:
  \begin{equation}
    q \leftarrow \min(q,\; \text{cap}), \qquad
    \text{cap} = \begin{cases}
      \lfloor (\text{cash}_i - \text{reserved\_cash}_i) / p \rfloor & \text{if } \text{side} = \code{"bid"}, \\
      \text{shares}_i - \text{reserved\_shares}_i & \text{if } \text{side} = \code{"ask"}.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  For a market buy (\code{Market.market(cid, "bid", q)}), \code{Market} walks the resting ask levels
  $(p_\ell, v_\ell)$ in ascending price order and computes the exact affordable quantity before
  submitting to the book, guaranteeing that settlement can never drive cash negative.
\item \textbf{Settlement (\code{Market.\_settle}):} For every trade $(p, \Delta q, \text{buyer } b,
  \text{seller } s)$ in \code{report["trades"]}:
  \begin{align}
    \text{cash}_b &\mathrel{-}= p\,\Delta q, & \text{shares}_b &\mathrel{+}= \Delta q, \\
    \text{cash}_s &\mathrel{+}= p\,\Delta q, & \text{shares}_s &\mathrel{-}= \Delta q.
  \end{align}
  If the incoming aggressor was a bid, the passive maker was a resting ask, so
  $\text{reserved\_shares}_s \mathrel{-}= \Delta q$; if the incoming aggressor was an ask, the passive
  maker was a resting bid at price $p$, so $\text{reserved\_cash}_b \mathrel{-}= p\,\Delta q$.
  Then, if $q_{\text{rest}} = \code{report["resting\_quantity"]} > 0$ rests on the book, the submitter's
  reservation is incremented ($\text{reserved\_cash}_c \mathrel{+}= p\,q_{\text{rest}}$ for a bid, or
  $\text{reserved\_shares}_c \mathrel{+}= q_{\text{rest}}$ for an ask). Finally, every order in
  \code{report["evicted\_orders"]} and \code{report["stp\_cancelled\_orders"]} has its reservation
  released via \code{\_release}.
\end{itemize}
This guarantees three global invariants checked by \code{Simulator.check\_conservation()}:
\begin{align}
  \sum_i \text{cash}_i &= \text{const}, &
  \sum_i \text{shares}_i &= \text{const}, \\
  \forall i:\quad \text{reserved\_cash}_i &= \!\!\sum_{o \in \text{bids}(i)}\!\! p_o q_o, &
  \text{reserved\_shares}_i &= \!\!\sum_{o \in \text{asks}(i)}\!\! q_o.
\end{align}

\subsection{The Counterfactual Visibility Switch (\code{Market.hide(cid)})}

In \code{Market.\_settle}, each fill updates two sets of step accumulators:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Total traded volume:} \code{\_buy\_volume} and \code{\_sell\_volume} always include every
  fill, regardless of who traded.
\item \textbf{Public flow accumulators:} \code{\_flow\_buy}, \code{\_flow\_sell}, and the anonymous step
  trade list \code{\_step\_trades} include a fill \emph{if and only if}
  $\code{report["client\_id"]} \notin \code{self.hidden\_aggressors}$.
\end{enumerate}
Calling \code{sim.market.hide(executor.cid)} adds the executor's client ID to
\code{hidden\_aggressors}. When hidden, the executor's orders still enter the book, match against
makers, transfer cash and shares, move the mid-price, and count toward total traded volume, \emph{but}
detectors and the public signed-flow ring buffer do not see the executor's aggressive trades. Comparing
a run with \code{visibility="visible"} against \code{visibility="invisible"} on the same seed isolates
the exact cost of detection (\S\ref{sec:e3}).

%==================================================================================================
\section{Rolling Public State (\code{order\_book.public\_state})}
\label{sec:public}
%==================================================================================================

At the end of every step $t$, \code{Market.end\_step()} calls
\code{PublicState.update(book, buy\_volume, sell\_volume, last, flow\_buy, flow\_sell, step\_trades)}.
\code{PublicState} (\code{order\_book/public\_state.py}) is the \textbf{only} market state object that
agents, detectors, and the executor are allowed to read.

Let $b_t, a_t$ be the best bid and best ask at the end of step $t$. If both sides are non-empty,
$m_t = (b_t + a_t)/2$ and $\text{spread}_t = a_t - b_t$. If one side is empty, $m_t$ falls back to the
last trade price $p_{\text{last}}$ (or the previous mid $m_{t-1}$ if no trade has occurred) and
$\text{spread}_t = 0$. Let $\Delta m_t = m_t - m_{t-1}$ be the one-step mid change, and
$V_t = V^{\text{buy}}_t + V^{\text{sell}}_t$ be total step volume.

\paragraph{1. Fast and slow EWMAs.} With fast span $\tau_f = 10$ ($\alpha_f = 2/(\tau_f+1) = 2/11$) and
slow span $\tau_s = 100$ ($\alpha_s = 2/(\tau_s+1) = 2/101$), \code{PublicState} updates six exponential
weighted moving averages in $O(1)$:
\begin{align}
  \bar m^{f}_t &= \bar m^{f}_{t-1} + \alpha_f (m_t - \bar m^{f}_{t-1}), &
  \bar m^{s}_t &= \bar m^{s}_{t-1} + \alpha_s (m_t - \bar m^{s}_{t-1}), \\
  \sigma^{2,f}_t &= \sigma^{2,f}_{t-1} + \alpha_f \big((\Delta m_t)^2 - \sigma^{2,f}_{t-1}\big), &
  \sigma^{2,s}_t &= \sigma^{2,s}_{t-1} + \alpha_s \big((\Delta m_t)^2 - \sigma^{2,s}_{t-1}\big), \\
  \bar V^{f}_t &= \bar V^{f}_{t-1} + \alpha_f (V_t - \bar V^{f}_{t-1}), &
  \bar V^{s}_t &= \bar V^{s}_{t-1} + \alpha_s (V_t - \bar V^{s}_{t-1}).
\end{align}

\paragraph{2. Multi-scale momentum indicators.} For each lookback pair
$(\ell_{f,k}, \ell_{s,k}) \in \{(5,30),\; (10,60),\; (20,120)\}$ ($k \in \{0,1,2\}$), two separate mid
EMAs $E^{(k,f)}_t$ and $E^{(k,s)}_t$ with weights $2/(\ell_{f,k}+1)$ and $2/(\ell_{s,k}+1)$ are updated,
yielding the three public momentum signals:
\begin{equation}
  \mu^{(k)}_t = E^{(k,f)}_t - E^{(k,s)}_t \quad\text{(in ticks)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{3. Ring-buffer order-flow imbalance.} Over a rolling window of $W = 20$ steps
(\code{flow\_window}), \code{PublicState} maintains two fixed-length ring buffers \code{\_buy[0..W-1]}
and \code{\_sell[0..W-1]} together with running sums $B_t = \sum_{u=t-W+1}^t V^{\text{flow,buy}}_u$ and
$S_t = \sum_{u=t-W+1}^t V^{\text{flow,sell}}_u$ (evicting slot $t \bmod W$ and adding the new step's
visible aggressive flow in $O(1)$). The public order-flow imbalance is:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:imbalance}
  I_t = \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{B_t - S_t}{B_t + S_t} \in [-1, 1] & \text{if } B_t + S_t > 0, \\[6pt]
    0.0 & \text{if } B_t + S_t = 0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{4. Attention multiplier (endogenous activity clustering).} Once the step counter reaches
$\tau_s = 100$ (\code{warmed\_up = True}), \code{PublicState} computes a bounded activity multiplier
that rises when fast volatility or fast volume exceeds its slow baseline:
\begin{equation}
  a_t = \min\!\Bigg(3.0,\; \max\!\Bigg(0.5,\;
  1.0 + 0.5\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2,f}_t}{\sigma^{2,s}_t}} - 1\bigg)
      + 0.5\bigg(\frac{\bar V^{f}_t}{\bar V^{s}_t} - 1\bigg)\Bigg)\Bigg),
\end{equation}
with $a_t = 1.0$ before warm-up. This creates realistic volatility and volume clustering without hard-coding
regime switches.

%==================================================================================================
\section{Randomness Architecture and Common Random Numbers (\code{order\_book.simulator})}
\label{sec:rng}
%==================================================================================================

To measure small leakage effects ($\sim 0.3\text{ bps}$) buried inside background price fluctuations whose
standard deviation over $2\,000$ steps is $\sim 10\text{ bps}$, the simulator must support \textbf{paired
counterfactuals with Common Random Numbers (CRN)}.

In \code{Simulator.\_\_init\_\_(seed, config, analysis)}, a single root
\code{np.random.SeedSequence(seed)} spawns a sequence of statistically independent PCG64 streams via
\code{new\_stream()}:
\begin{align*}
  \underbrace{\text{Stream 0}}_{\text{Scheduler}} \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{Stream 1}}_{\text{Fundamental } v_t} \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{Streams } 2..5}_{\text{Market Makers}} \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{Stream 6}}_{\text{NoiseFlow}} \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{Streams } 7..6+n_{\text{inst}}}_{\text{Institutions}} \\
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{Next Stream}}_{\text{Speculators}} \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{Next}}_{\text{Anticipators}} \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{Next}}_{\text{Externals}}.
\end{align*}
This design provides four mathematical guarantees verified in \code{tests/test\_foundations.py}:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Bitwise determinism:} Running \code{Simulator(seed, cfg)} twice produces identical order
  IDs, trade prices, quantities, and mid paths down to the last bit.
\item \textbf{Snapshot/restore equivalence:} \code{blob = sim.snapshot()} (\code{pickle.dumps}) followed
  by \code{Simulator.restore(blob)} preserves the exact internal state of every PCG64 bit generator.
\item \textbf{In-place reseeding:} \code{sim.reseed(new\_seed)} spawns a new \code{SeedSequence(new\_seed)}
  and overwrites \code{stream.bit\_generator.state} in place for every existing stream, so every agent
  holding a reference to its stream immediately follows the new seed.
\item \textbf{Counterfactual pairing:} Because external agents (\code{add\_external}) are appended at the
  end of the stream list and act outside the scheduler's permutation, two runs on the same seed (e.g.\
  visible vs.\ invisible executor, or with vs.\ without an injected metaorder) share the exact same
  random numbers for the hidden fundamental value, the scheduler's permutation, and every background
  agent up to the step where the book state itself first differs.
\end{enumerate}

%==================================================================================================
\section{Mathematical Specification of Every Background Agent (\code{order\_book.agents})}
\label{sec:agents}
%==================================================================================================

All agents inherit from \code{Agent(cid, market, rng, cash, shares)} in \code{order\_book/agents/base.py}
(except \code{Speculators}, which vectorises $n=40$ accounts inside one object for speed). Below are the
exact mathematical equations implemented in each file.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Hidden Fundamental Value (\code{agents/fundamental.py})}
\label{sec:fundamental}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the default \emph{shared-value} mode (\code{FundamentalConfig.private = False}), the exogenous
fundamental value $v_t$ (initialised at $v_0 = m_0 = 10\,000$ ticks) evolves at the start of step $t$ as
an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck / Gaussian random walk:
\begin{equation}
  v_t = v_{t-1} + \rho_v (v_0 - v_{t-1}) + \sigma_v \varepsilon_t,
  \qquad \varepsilon_t \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1),
\end{equation}
with default $\sigma_v = 0.3$ ticks/step and $\rho_v = 0.0$.

In the alternative \emph{private-valuations} mode (\code{private = True}), there is no common anchor:
each speculator $i \in \{1,\dots,n_{\text{spec}}\}$ maintains its own private valuation $v_{i,t}$ that
adapts toward the previous step's public mid-price $m_{t-1}$:
\begin{equation}
  v_{i,t} = v_{i,t-1} + \kappa_a (m_{t-1} - v_{i,t-1}) + \sigma_v \varepsilon_{i,t},
  \qquad \varepsilon_{i,t} \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1),
\end{equation}
with default $\kappa_a = 0.01$. Notice that in private mode \code{FundamentalValue.advance()} still draws
one normal variate from its own stream each step so that stream alignment is preserved.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Zero-Intelligence Noise Flow (\code{agents/noise.py})}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\code{NoiseFlow} represents retail and liquidity-motivated background traders using a single pooled
account (endowment $6\,000$ shares and $6\,000 \times m_0$ cash). On each step $t$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Random cancellation:} Let $R_t$ be the list of currently resting order IDs owned by the
  noise pool. Draw $K_t \sim \text{Binomial}(|R_t|,\; p_{\text{cancel}})$ with $p_{\text{cancel}} = 0.015$,
  sample $K_t$ order IDs uniformly without replacement from $R_t$, and cancel them.
\item \textbf{Attention-modulated Poisson arrivals:} Draw the number of new orders
  \begin{equation}
    N_t \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_{\text{noise}}\, a_t), \qquad \lambda_{\text{noise}} = 2.5,
  \end{equation}
  where $a_t \in [0.5, 3.0]$ is the public attention multiplier.
\item \textbf{Wealth-balanced side probability:} To prevent the noise pool from drifting into cash or
  share exhaustion over long simulations, compute its current cash share of mark-to-market wealth:
  \begin{equation}
    c_t = \frac{\text{cash}_t}{\text{cash}_t + \text{shares}_t \cdot m_t}, \qquad
    p_{\text{bid},t} = 0.5 + w_{\text{bal}}(c_t - 0.5), \qquad w_{\text{bal}} = 0.5.
  \end{equation}
  If cash is high ($c_t > 0.5$), $p_{\text{bid},t} > 0.5$ so the pool buys more often; if shares are high
  ($c_t < 0.5$), it sells more often.
\item \textbf{Order size, type, and price placement:} For each of the $i = 1,\dots,N_t$ orders, draw size
  $q_i \sim \text{Uniform}\{1, 2, \dots, q_{\max}\}$ ($q_{\max} = 12$), type selector
  $u_i \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$, and price offset
  $d_i \sim \text{Geometric}\big(p_{\text{geom}} = \frac{1}{\bar d + 1}\big)$ on $\{1,2,\dots\}$ with
  $\bar d = 3$ (\code{mean\_offset}):
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item If $u_i < f_{\text{mkt}} = 0.15$: submit a \textbf{market order} of half size
    $\lfloor (q_i + 1)/2 \rfloor$ (reflecting the empirical fact that aggressive takers use smaller order
    sizes than passive liquidity providers).
  \item Else if $u_i < f_{\text{mkt}} + f_{\text{cross}} = 0.20$: submit a \textbf{marketable limit
    order} of size $q_i$ priced right at the opposite best ($p = a_t$ for a bid, $p = b_t$ for an ask).
  \item Else ($80\%$ of orders): submit a \textbf{passive limit order} of size $q_i$ placed $d_i$ ticks
    behind the opposite best:
    \begin{equation}
      p_i = \begin{cases}
        a_t - d_i & \text{if buying (\code{"bid"})}, \\
        b_t + d_i & \text{if selling (\code{"ask"})}.
      \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection[Fundamentalists, Chartists, and Brock--Hommes]{Fundamentalists and Chartists (\code{agents/speculators.py})}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\code{Speculators} models $n = 40$ boundedly rational traders (endowment $400$ shares each, position
cap $P_{\max} = 400$ shares relative to endowment). At initialization, each member $i \in \{0,\dots,39\}$
draws:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item a permanent private valuation bias $\beta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{bias}}^2)$ with
  $\sigma_{\text{bias}} = 3.0$ ticks;
\item an initial strategy mode $M_{i,0} \in \{0\text{ (fundamentalist)},\; 1\text{ (chartist)}\}$
  uniformly;
\item a momentum lookback index $k_i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ uniformly (corresponding to EMA pairs
  $(5,30), (10,60), (20,120)$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Step activation.} On each step $t$, each speculator independently becomes active with
probability $p_{\text{act}} = 0.05$ (on average 2 active speculators per step). An active speculator $i$
first cancels all of its resting orders (\code{m.cancel\_all(cid)}) and computes its net inventory
$x_{i,t} = \text{shares}_{i,t} - \text{shares}_{i,0}$.

\paragraph{Mode $M_{i,t} = 0$: Fundamentalist.} Forms a noisy perception of fundamental value:
\begin{equation}
  \hat v_{i,t} = \tilde v_{i,t} + \beta_i + \eta_{i,t}, \qquad \eta_{i,t} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{obs}}^2),
  \quad \sigma_{\text{obs}} = 1.0\text{ tick},
\end{equation}
where $\tilde v_{i,t} = v_t$ in shared-value mode and $v_{i,t}$ in private-valuation mode. Define the
mispricing gap $g_{i,t} = \hat v_{i,t} - m_t$.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item If $|g_{i,t}| < \theta_f = 2.0$ ticks: do nothing.
\item Otherwise, trade in direction $\text{side} = \code{"bid"}$ if $g_{i,t} > 0$ else $\code{"ask"}$,
  with desired quantity capped by both $q_{f,\max} = 20$ and remaining position room:
  \begin{equation}
    q = \min\!\big(q_{f,\max},\; \max(1, \operatorname{round}(|g_{i,t}| - \theta_f)),\;
    P_{\max} - \sign(g_{i,t})\,x_{i,t}\big).
  \end{equation}
\item If $|g_{i,t}| \ge g_{\text{aggr}} = 8.0$ ticks, submit a \textbf{market order} for $q$. Otherwise
  submit a \textbf{passive limit order} priced at $\min(\lfloor \hat v_{i,t} - \theta_f \rfloor, a_t - 1)$
  for a buy or $\max(\lceil \hat v_{i,t} + \theta_f \rceil, b_t + 1)$ for a sell.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mode $M_{i,t} = 1$: Chartist (trend follower).} Reads its assigned momentum signal
$\mu = \mu^{(k_i)}_t$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Exit rule:} If the trader holds a non-zero position ($x_{i,t} \neq 0$) and the trend has
  either reversed ($x_{i,t}\cdot\mu < 0$) or faded ($|\mu| < 0.25\,\theta_c$), it unwinds toward zero by
  submitting a market order of size $\min(|x_{i,t}|, q_{c,\max})$ in the opposite direction.
\item \textbf{Entry rule:} If $|\mu| \ge \theta_c = 6.0$ ticks, it submits a market order in the
  direction of $\sign(\mu)$ with size
  \begin{equation}
    q = \min\!\big(q_{c,\max},\; \max(1, \operatorname{round}(s_c(|\mu| - \theta_c))),\;
    P_{\max} - \sign(\mu)\,x_{i,t}\big),
  \end{equation}
  where $q_{c,\max} = 10$ lots and $s_c = 2.0$ lots/tick.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Brock--Hommes evolutionary mode switching.} Every $T_{\text{switch}} = 50$ steps, let
$W_{i,t} = \text{cash}_{i,t} + \text{shares}_{i,t} m_t$ be each speculator's mark-to-market wealth and
$r_{i,t} = 100 \times (W_{i,t} - W_{i,t-50}) / W_{i,t-50}$ be its percentage return over the 50-step
block. The fitness $F_m$ of each mode $m \in \{0,1\}$ is updated via an EWMA with memory
$\lambda_F = 0.1$:
\begin{equation}
  F_m \leftarrow F_m + \lambda_F \big(\bar r_m - F_m\big), \qquad
  \bar r_m = \frac{1}{|\{i : M_{i} = m\}|} \sum_{i : M_i = m} r_{i,t},
\end{equation}
and every speculator re-draws its mode $M_{i,t+1} = 1$ (chartist) with logistic probability
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{P}(M_{i,t+1} = 1) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\big(-\beta_{\text{BH}}(F_1 - F_0)\big)},
  \qquad \beta_{\text{BH}} = 2.0.
\end{equation}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection[Institutional Traders and Metaorders]{Institutional Traders (\code{agents/institutional.py})}
\label{sec:institutions}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The population contains $n_{\text{inst}}$ background institutional traders ($4$ in full-cover regimes,
$1$ in \code{low\_cover}, $0$ in \code{no\_cover}). Their economic role is crucial: because they execute
large parent orders using standard industry schedules, they generate legitimate one-sided order flow that
creates \textbf{false positives} for detectors and provides \textbf{statistical cover} for the RL
executor.

\paragraph{Metaorder arrival and parameters.} While an institutional trader is idle, it starts a new
metaorder on step $t$ with probability $\lambda_{\text{inst}} = 5\times 10^{-4}$ (mean idle time $2\,000$
steps). Upon arrival:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Inventory-reverting side choice:} With normalized inventory
  $x_t = (\text{shares}_t - \text{shares}_0)/\text{shares}_0$, the metaorder is a buy (\code{"bid"})
  with probability
  \begin{equation}
    p_{\text{bid}} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}\tanh(\pi_I x_t), \qquad \pi_I = 2.0.
  \end{equation}
\item \textbf{Log-normal size:} Draw $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and set
  \begin{equation}
    Q = \min\!\big(Q_{\max},\; \max(1, \operatorname{round}(Q_{\text{med}} \exp(\sigma_Q Z))),\;
    \text{affordable lots}\big),
  \end{equation}
  with $Q_{\text{med}} = 600$ lots, $\sigma_Q = 0.7$, and $Q_{\max} = 3\,000$ lots.
\item \textbf{Participation rate and duration:} Draw target participation
  $\pi \sim \text{Uniform}(0.02, 0.20)$. Using the public slow volume EWMA $\bar V^s_t$ (or
  $V_{\text{fallback}} = 5.0$ lots/step before warm-up), the planned horizon is
  \begin{equation}
    T = \clamp\!\Big(\operatorname{round}\!\Big(\frac{Q}{\pi\,\bar V^s_t}\Big),\; 100,\; 4\,000\Big)\text{ steps}.
  \end{equation}
\item \textbf{Execution style:} Drawn from the 5 styles (\code{twap\_market}, \code{twap\_random},
  \code{twap\_passive}, \code{pov}, \code{front\_loaded}) with child decision interval $\Delta = 5$ steps.
  At $t = t_{\text{start}} + T$, any remaining unfilled quantity is forced through by market order.
\end{enumerate}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection[Market Makers with Detection and Fragility]{Avellaneda--Stoikov Market Makers (\code{agents/market\_maker.py})}
\label{sec:mm}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each of the $n_{\text{mm}} = 4$ market makers quotes both a bid and an ask on every step, managing its
inventory $I_t = \text{shares}_t - \text{shares}_0$ within $[-I_{\max}, I_{\max}]$ ($I_{\max} = 200$).

\paragraph{1. Volatility fragility and pull cooldown.} Let the fast-to-slow volatility ratio be
$\varrho_t = \sqrt{\sigma^{2,f}_t / \sigma^{2,s}_t}$. If $\varrho_t > \varrho_{\text{pull}} = 3.0$, the
maker enters a pull state for $T_{\text{cool}} = 20$ steps ($\text{pulled\_until} = t + 20$), cancelling
both quotes immediately and staying out of the book until $t \ge \text{pulled\_until}$. Otherwise, its
base quote size is scaled by the fragility factor:
\begin{equation}
  f_{\text{frag},t} = \max\!\big(0.0,\; 1.0 - \phi\,\max(\varrho_t - 1.0, 0.0)\big), \qquad \phi = 0.5.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{2. Avellaneda--Stoikov reservation price and half-spread.} With effective variance
$\sigma^2_t = \max(\sigma^{2,f}_t, \sigma^2_{\min})$ ($\sigma^2_{\min} = 0.25$), risk aversion
$\gamma = 0.003$, liquidity parameter $\kappa = 1.0$, horizon $H = 50$, and cap $\bar s = 10.0$ ticks:
\begin{align}
  \text{Risk term:}\quad R_t &= \gamma\,\sigma^2_t\,H, \\
  \text{Reservation price:}\quad r_t &= m_t - \clamp(I_t\,R_t,\; -\bar s,\; +\bar s), \\
  \text{Half-spread:}\quad h_t &= \clamp\!\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(R_t + \frac{2}{\gamma}\ln\!\big(1 + \frac{\gamma}{\kappa}\big)\Big),\; 1.0,\; \bar s\Big).
\end{align}

\paragraph{3. Detector-driven defensive response (the source of leakage cost).} If the market maker is
wired to a detector (\code{cfg.detector is not None}), it reads the detector's signed signal
$s_t \in [-1, 1]$. When $s_t > 0$, a buy metaorder is suspected, so the \textbf{pressured side} is the
\code{"ask"} (buyers lift asks); when $s_t < 0$, the pressured side is the \code{"bid"}:
\begin{align}
  r_t &\leftarrow r_t + \delta_{\text{skew}}\,s_t, && (\delta_{\text{skew}} = 4.0\text{ ticks}), \\
  \omega_{\text{pressured}} &= \delta_{\text{widen}}\,|s_t|,\quad \omega_{\text{other}} = 0, && (\delta_{\text{widen}} = 3.0\text{ ticks}), \\
  \text{scale}_{\text{pressured}} &= f_{\text{frag},t}\cdot\max(0.0,\; 1.0 - \delta_{\text{cut}}\,|s_t|), && (\delta_{\text{cut}} = 0.7), \\
  \text{scale}_{\text{other}} &= f_{\text{frag},t}.
\end{align}
Target quote prices are then $p_{\text{bid}} = r_t - h_t - \omega_{\text{bid}}$ and
$p_{\text{ask}} = \max(r_t + h_t + \omega_{\text{ask}},\; p_{\text{bid}} + 2)$, clamped to be strictly
post-only against all other participants' best quotes via \code{book.best\_excluding(side, own\_oid)}.

\paragraph{4. Queue-position preservation.} A resting quote on side $S \in \{\text{bid}, \text{ask}\}$ is
kept untouched (preserving its FIFO queue priority!) if and only if
$|p_{\text{resting}} - p_{\text{target}}| < 1.0\text{ tick}$ and its remaining quantity satisfies
$0.5\,q_{\text{target}} \le q_{\text{resting}} \le q_{\text{target}}$. Otherwise it is cancelled and
replaced with a freshly drawn base size $q_{\text{base}} \sim \text{Uniform}\{5,\dots,20\}$ multiplied by
$\text{scale}_S$.

\subsection{Order Anticipator (\code{agents/anticipator.py})}
\label{sec:anticipator}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Present under adversary level \code{A4}, \code{OrderAnticipator} reads $s_t = \text{detector.signal}$ and
trades aggressively with market orders:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item If $|s_t| \ge \theta_A = 0.5$: increment \code{confident\_steps} and trade in direction
  $d = \sign(s_t)$ (or $-d$ for the first \code{switch\_after} $= 50$ steps if
  \code{contrarian\_first=True}) with market order quantity
  \begin{equation}
    q = \min\!\Big(c_{\max},\; \operatorname{round}\!\Big(\varphi\,\frac{|s_t| - \theta_A}{1 - \theta_A}\Big),\;
    P_{\max} - d\cdot x_t\Big), \qquad (\varphi = 2.0,\; c_{\max} = 10,\; P_{\max} = 200).
  \end{equation}
\item If $|s_t| < \theta_{\text{fade}} = 0.2$: reset \code{confident\_steps} $= 0$ and unwind any non-zero
  inventory $x_t$ by submitting a market order of size $\min(c_{\max}, |x_t|)$ in direction $-\sign(x_t)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Simulator Validation: Stylised Facts and Square-Root Impact (Phase 1)}

Before any execution algorithm or detector is evaluated, the background market (\code{A0}, no executor)
must reproduce the empirical regularities (``stylised facts'') of real limit order books and the concave

\subsection{Paired-Injection Square-Root Impact Methodology (\code{order\_book.impact})}
\label{sec:impact}

To measure the mechanical impact law $\Delta m \propto (Q/V)^\delta$ cleanly without the noise of the
background price random walk ($\sigma \sqrt{T} \approx 8$--$12$ ticks over $T=2\,000$ steps),
\code{impact.py} uses Common Random Numbers (\S\ref{sec:rng}):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item For each seed, run a reference background simulation after a $2\,000$-step burn-in for
  $T_{\text{ref}} + T_{\text{after}} = 2\,500$ steps and record its mid-price path $m^{\text{bg}}_t$ and
  mean step volume $\bar V$.
\item For each participation fraction $f \in \{0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, run the identical seed
  with an external \code{InstitutionalTrader} (\code{add\_external}) that injects a single buy metaorder
  of size $Q = f \cdot \bar V \cdot T_{\text{ref}}$ executed by \code{twap\_market} over $T_{\text{ref}} =
  2\,000$ steps, yielding mid path $m^{(f)}_t$.
\item The paired impact path $\Delta m^{(f)}_t = m^{(f)}_t - m^{\text{bg}}_t$ cancels the background
  price drift. From $\Delta m^{(f)}_t$ we read impact at completion ($t = T_{\text{ref}}$), peak impact
  ($\max_{t \le T_{\text{ref}}} \Delta m^{(f)}_t$), and residual impact $500$ steps after completion
  ($t = T_{\text{ref}} + 500$), and fit the power-law exponent $\delta$ by OLS of $\log(\text{impact})$
  on $\log(f)$.
\end{enumerate}

\section[Specification of All Detectors]{Specification of All Detectors (\code{order\_book.detectors})}
\label{sec:detectors}
%==================================================================================================

Every detector implements \code{Detector.update(public: PublicState) -> float} and exposes
$\code{signal} \in [-1,1]$, $\code{p\_buy} \in [0,1]$, and $\code{p\_sell} \in [0,1]$.

\subsection{Level A1: Rule-Based Flow Imbalance Detector (\code{detectors/flow.py})}

Maintains an EWMA $M_t$ of the 20-step signed order-flow imbalance $I_t$ (\ref{eq:imbalance}) with memory
span $\tau_d = 10$ ($\alpha_d = 2/11$):
\begin{equation}
  M_t = M_{t-1} + \frac{2}{\tau_d + 1}(I_t - M_{t-1}), \qquad
  s_t = \sign(M_t)\cdot\min\!\Big(1.0,\; \max\!\Big(0.0,\; \frac{|M_t| - \theta_d}{1 - \theta_d}\Big)\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\theta_d \in \{0.20\text{ (lenient)},\; 0.30\text{ (medium)},\; 0.45\text{ (strict)}\}$.

\subsection{Public Feature Tracker (\code{detectors/features.py})}

\code{FeatureTracker} maintains a 200-step deque of per-step flow tuples
$(V^{\text{buy}}_t, V^{\text{sell}}_t, N^{\text{trades}}_t, N^{\text{buy\_trades}}_t)$, a volume-clock
deque of the most recent aggressive trades summing to at most $V_{\text{clock}} = 100$ lots, a 51-step
mid-price deque, and the current same-sign aggressive trade run (sign, length, and last 10 trade sizes).
On each step it outputs the 10-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{f}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$:
\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{clL{9.4cm}}
\toprule
Index & Name & Exact Formula \\
\midrule
$f_0$ & \code{imbalance\_20} & 20-step volume imbalance $I^{(20)}_t = (B_{20} - S_{20})/(B_{20} + S_{20})$ \\
$f_1$ & \code{imbalance\_50} & 50-step volume imbalance $I^{(50)}_t = (B_{50} - S_{50})/(B_{50} + S_{50})$ \\
$f_2$ & \code{imbalance\_200} & 200-step volume imbalance $I^{(200)}_t = (B_{200} - S_{200})/(B_{200} + S_{200})$ \\
$f_3$ & \code{imbalance\_volume\_clock} & Signed volume imbalance over the last $100$ traded lots \\
$f_4$ & \code{signed\_run} & $\text{run\_sign}_t \cdot \min(\text{run\_length}_t, 20) / 10 \in [-2, 2]$ \\
$f_5$ & \code{regularity} & $\max(0, 1 - \text{std}(\text{run\_sizes})/\text{mean}(\text{run\_sizes}))$ if $\text{run\_length}_t \ge 3$ else $0$ \\
$f_6$ & \code{trade\_rate\_ratio} & $(\text{trades in last 20 steps}/20) / (\text{trades in last 200 steps}/200)$ \\
$f_7$ & \code{normalised\_return\_50} & $(m_t - m_{t-50}) / \sqrt{\max(\sigma^{2,f}_t, 10^{-6}) \cdot 50}$ \\
$f_8$ & \code{spread} & Current bid--ask spread $a_t - b_t$ (ticks) \\
$f_9$ & \code{count\_imbalance\_20} & 20-step trade-count imbalance $(N^{\text{buy}}_{20} - N^{\text{sell}}_{20}) / N^{\text{total}}_{20}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Level A2: Four Held-Out Statistical Detectors (\code{detectors/statistical.py})}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{\code{RunsDetector}:} Fires on persistent streaks of same-sign aggressive trades:
  \begin{equation}
    s_t = \text{run\_sign}_t \cdot \clip\!\Big(\frac{\text{run\_length}_t - 4}{6},\; 0,\; 1\Big).
  \end{equation}
\item \textbf{\code{CusumDetector}:} Two-sided Page's CUSUM with restart on non-overlapping 20-step
  blocks. At the end of each 20-step block with block imbalance $x_b$:
  \begin{equation}
    S^+_b = \max(0, S^+_{b-1} + x_b - k), \qquad S^-_b = \max(0, S^-_{b-1} - x_b - k), \qquad (k = 0.1).
  \end{equation}
  When $S^+_b > h = 1.5$ or $S^-_b > h = 1.5$, an alarm is triggered ($\text{alarm\_sign} = \pm 1$), both
  $S^+_b, S^-_b$ reset to $0$, and the alarm signal decays linearly over $H_{\text{hold}} = 10$ blocks:
  $s_t = \text{alarm\_sign}\cdot\max(0, 1 - b_{\text{since}}/10)$ (or $(S^+_b - S^-_b)/h$ if larger in
  magnitude).
\item \textbf{\code{VolumeClockDetector}:} Applies the A1 dead-zone threshold ($\theta = 0.3$) to the
  100-lot volume-clock imbalance $f_3$ instead of wall-clock time.
\item \textbf{\code{RegularityDetector} (miniature Goliath--Gebbie metaorder reconstruction):} Detects
  child-order splitting that is both persistent ($\text{run\_length}_t \ge 3$) and suspiciously uniform
  in slice size:
  \begin{equation}
    s_t = \mathbb{I}(\text{run\_length}_t \ge 3)\cdot \text{run\_sign}_t \cdot f_{5,t} \cdot
    \min\!\Big(1.0,\; \frac{\text{run\_length}_t}{6}\Big).
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Level A3: Learned Multinomial Logistic Classifier (\code{detectors/learned.py})}

Trained offline by \code{scripts/train\_detector.py} on background runs (40 training seeds $\times$
$20\,000$ steps, 8 validation seeds) where only background institutions trade. Each step $t$ is labelled
from \code{MetaorderLog} as $y_t \in \{0\text{ (none)},\; 1\text{ (net buy active)},\; 2\text{ (net sell
active)}\}$. Standardised features $\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_t = (\mathbf{f}_t - \boldsymbol{\mu})/\boldsymbol{\sigma}$
feed a 3-class linear layer $\mathbf{z}_t = \tilde{\mathbf{f}}_t \mathbf{W} + \mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^3$
with softmax probabilities $\mathbf{p}_t = \operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_t)$ and signed signal
$s_t = p_{t,1} - p_{t,2}$. Fitted weights (saved in \code{outputs/detectors/learned.npz}) place positive
weight on multi-horizon imbalances ($f_0, f_1, f_2, f_3$), run length ($f_4$), size regularity ($f_5$),
spread ($f_8$), and count imbalance ($f_9$).

  achieve $\text{AUC} \approx 0.55$--$0.60$ on institutional metaorders, but $\text{AUC} \approx 0.47$--$0.51$
  (chance level) at distinguishing the paced executor from institutional-only steps, with institutional
  false-alarm rates of $12\%$--$27\%$. In short: \textbf{four background institutions provide so much
  natural cover that a $5\%$--$20\%$ TWAP is already hidden}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Formulation of the Execution MDP}
\label{sec:mdp}
%==================================================================================================

An execution episode has horizon $T = 2\,000$ simulator steps and decision interval $N = 5$ steps, so the
MDP has at most $K = T/N = 400$ discrete decision stages $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, K-1\}$ occurring at
simulator steps $t_k = t_{\text{start}} + kN$ (terminating earlier if the parent order of size $Q$ is
completely filled before $T$).

\subsection{Observation Space ($\mathcal{S} \subset \mathbb{R}^{13}$, \code{execution/features.py})}

At decision stage $k$, \code{features(s: ExecState)} maps the executor's public state and own progress
into a 13-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{o}_k = (o_{k,0},\dots,o_{k,12})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{13}$.
Every directional market feature is multiplied by $\sign \in \{+1\text{ (buy)}, -1\text{ (sell)}\}$ so
that a single policy network is invariant to whether it is buying or selling:

\subsection{Discrete Factored Action Space ($\mathcal{A} = \{0,\dots,14\}$, \code{execution/features.py})}

The action space is the Cartesian product of 5 pace multipliers and 3 order placements,
$|\mathcal{A}| = 5 \times 3 = 15$:
\begin{align}
  \code{SIZE\_MULTS} &= (0.0,\; 0.5,\; 1.0,\; 1.5,\; 2.5), \\
  \code{PLACEMENTS} &= (\code{"market"},\; \code{"passive\_best"},\; \code{"passive\_behind"}).
\end{align}
Given action index $a \in \{0,\dots,14\}$ corresponding to pair $(m_a, \text{place}_a)$, let the
remaining-over-decisions-left catch-up pace be $b_k = R_k / S_k$ and set the child quantity
\begin{equation}
  q_k = \max(0,\; \operatorname{round}(m_a \cdot b_k)).
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item If $q_k = 0$ (which holds for all three actions with $m_a = 0.0$, indices $0, 1, 2$): return
  \code{Decision()} (do nothing; leave any existing resting orders in place to preserve their FIFO
  queue position!).
\item If $q_k > 0$ and $\text{place}_a = \code{"market"}$: return
  \code{Decision(cancel=True, market\_qty=$q_k$)}.
\item If $q_k > 0$ and $\text{place}_a = \code{"passive\_best"}$: return
  \code{Decision(cancel=True, limit\_qty=$q_k$, limit\_price=None)} (placed at own best: $b_t$ for a
  buy, $a_t$ for a sell).
\item If $q_k > 0$ and $\text{place}_a = \code{"passive\_behind"}$: return
  \code{Decision(cancel=True, limit\_qty=$q_k$, limit\_price=s.behind(1))} (placed 1 tick behind own
  best: $b_t - 1$ for a buy, $a_t + 1$ for a sell).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Telescoping Implementation-Shortfall Reward (\code{execution/env.py})}

A well-known pitfall in RL for optimal execution is reward mismatch: if step rewards include arbitrary
heuristic penalties, maximizing cumulative reward no longer minimizes implementation shortfall. In
\code{ExecutionEnv.rollout}, every individual fill $(t_f, p_f, q_f, \text{aggr}_f)$ recorded in
\code{executor.result.fills} between decision $k$ and decision $k+1$ (including any forced market fill at
the horizon $T$, which is credited to the final recorded transition $K_{\text{last}}$) contributes
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:step_reward}
  r_k = -\sum_{f \in \text{fills}(k)}
  \underbrace{\Big(\sign \cdot \frac{p_f - m_{\text{arrival}}}{m_{\text{arrival}}} \times 10^4\Big)}_{\text{fill shortfall in bps}}
  \cdot \frac{q_f}{Q}
  \;-\; \mathbb{I}(k = K_{\text{last}})\cdot \lambda_{\text{inc}} \frac{Q_{\text{incomplete}}}{Q},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{inc}} = 5.0\text{ bps}$.

\paragraph{Theorem (Exact Telescoping Return).} \emph{Whenever the order completes in full
($Q_{\text{incomplete}} = 0$, which includes any quantity forced by market order at $T$ so that
$\sum_{k} \sum_{f \in \text{fills}(k)} q_f = Q$), the undiscounted episode return equals the exact
negative implementation shortfall in basis points:}
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{k=0}^{K_{\text{last}}} r_k
  = -\frac{\sign}{m_{\text{arrival}}} \times 10^4 \sum_{k=0}^{K_{\text{last}}} \sum_{f \in \text{fills}(k)}
    (p_f - m_{\text{arrival}})\frac{q_f}{Q}
  = -\sign\cdot\frac{\bar p - m_{\text{arrival}}}{m_{\text{arrival}}} \times 10^4
  = -\text{IS (bps)}.
\end{equation}
This identity is verified to $10^{-6}$ in \code{tests/test\_rl.py::test\_rollout\_reward\_equals\_negative\_shortfall}.

\section{Actor--Critic Network and PPO Training Algorithm (\code{policy.py}, \code{ppo.py})}
\label{sec:ppo}
%==================================================================================================

\subsection{Network Architecture (\code{PolicyNet} and \code{PolicyStrategy})}

\code{PolicyNet} (\code{order\_book/execution/policy.py}) is a two-hidden-layer $\tanh$ multi-layer
perceptron with hidden dimension $H = 64$ ($6\,096$ trainable parameters) and separate linear actor and
critic heads:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_1 &= \tanh(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{o}_k + \mathbf{b}_1) \in \mathbb{R}^{64}, \\
  \mathbf{h}_2 &= \tanh(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{h}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2) \in \mathbb{R}^{64}, \\
\begin{align}
  \text{Action logits:}\quad \boldsymbol{\ell}_k &= \mathbf{W}_{\pi} \mathbf{h}_2 + \mathbf{b}_{\pi} \in \mathbb{R}^{15}, \\
  \pi_\theta(a \mid \mathbf{o}_k) &= \operatorname{Categorical}(\text{logits}=\boldsymbol{\ell}_k), \\
  \text{State value:}\quad V_\theta(\mathbf{o}_k) &= \mathbf{w}_V^\top \mathbf{h}_2 + b_V \in \mathbb{R}.
\end{align}
During training (\code{deterministic=False}, \code{recorder} active), \code{PolicyStrategy.decide}
samples $a_k \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid \mathbf{o}_k)$ using a local \code{torch.Generator} seeded by an
integer drawn from the executor's own PCG64 stream (\code{rng.integers(1 << 31)}), ensuring that
stochastic rollouts are $100\%$ deterministic given the simulator seed. During evaluation
(\code{deterministic=True}), it selects the greedy action $a_k = \arg\max_a \ell_{k,a}$.

\subsection[GAE and Clipped PPO]{GAE and Clipped PPO (\code{execution/ppo.py})}

Each PPO iteration $i \in \{1,\dots,I\}$ rolls out $M = 32$ full episodes on seeds drawn from the
training pool $\code{TRAIN\_SEEDS} = \{0,\dots,999\}$. For each episode trajectory
$(\mathbf{o}_0, a_0, r_0, \dots, \mathbf{o}_{K-1}, a_{K-1}, r_{K-1})$ with terminal bootstrap value
$V_K = 0$, \code{\_gae} computes the backward recurrence ($\gamma = 0.999$, $\lambda_{\text{GAE}} = 0.95$):
\begin{equation}
  \delta_k = r_k + \gamma V_\theta(\mathbf{o}_{k+1}) - V_\theta(\mathbf{o}_k), \qquad
  \hat A_k = \delta_k + \gamma\lambda_{\text{GAE}} \hat A_{k+1}, \qquad
  \hat R_k = \hat A_k + V_\theta(\mathbf{o}_k).
\end{equation}
Across all $N_{\text{batch}} \approx 32 \times 400 = 12\,800$ transitions collected in the iteration,
advantages are standardised: $\tilde A_k = (\hat A_k - \bar A)/(\operatorname{std}(\hat A) + 10^{-8})$.
Then \code{PPOTrainer.update} runs $E$ epochs (\code{epochs} $\in \{4, 10\}$) of shuffled minibatch
updates (minibatch size $B = 512$, Adam optimiser with learning rate $\text{lr}$ and global gradient-norm
clipping at $0.5$) minimising the combined loss:
\begin{align}
  \rho_k(\theta) &= \exp\!\big(\log \pi_\theta(a_k \mid \mathbf{o}_k) - \log \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_k \mid \mathbf{o}_k)\big), \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) &= -\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{k \in B}
    \min\!\Big(\rho_k(\theta)\,\tilde A_k,\; \clip(\rho_k(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\,\tilde A_k\Big), \qquad (\epsilon = 0.2), \\
  \mathcal{L}_V(\theta) &= \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{k \in B} \big(V_\theta(\mathbf{o}_k) - \hat R_k\big)^2, \qquad
  \mathcal{H}(\theta) = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{k \in B} H\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid \mathbf{o}_k)\big), \\
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) + c_V \mathcal{L}_V(\theta) - c_H \mathcal{H}(\theta),
  \qquad (c_V = 0.5,\; c_H = \code{entropy\_coef}).
\end{align}

\paragraph{Discussion of the Phase~3 RL results.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Consistent cost reduction across both order sizes:} At $Q/V = 0.20$, the learned policy
  reduces implementation shortfall from $+2.82\text{ bps}$ (TWAP) to $+2.40\text{ bps}$ ($-0.42\text{ bps}$,
  a $14.9\%$ total cost reduction, exceeding the $+0.31\text{ bps}$ pure leakage cost by also capturing
  spread and timing savings via passive orders). At $Q/V = 0.40$, the learned policy reduces IS from
  $+5.12\text{ bps}$ to $+4.74\text{ bps}$ ($-0.38\text{ bps}$) at $98.4\%$ natural completion and
  $+4.80\text{ bps}$ ($-0.32\text{ bps}$) at $99.7\%$ natural completion.
\item \textbf{Hyperparameter lessons:} Four epochs per rollout batch ($\code{epochs}=4$) is much more
  stable late in training than $\code{epochs}=10$: with $10$ epochs and a high learning rate, the policy
  can over-optimise a single rollout batch and drift toward lower pre-horizon completion after iteration
  $200$, which is why \code{train\_rl.py} saves \code{best\_policy.pt} at the best held-out evaluation
  alongside the final \code{policy.pt}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{description}[nosep]

  reported evictions, FIFO and partial fills on both sides, price protection of crossing limits, all four

  rejection of invalid modifications, input validation including NumPy integers, snapshot/array isolation,
  overflow of aggregate volume, and a 600-action random fuzz sequence asserting full book invariants
  after every single action.

  (fill, cancel, STP, window eviction, budget cap) and ring-buffer flow window updates.

  \code{reseed}, analysis-mode vs.\ training-mode logging, population construction with shared detector
  registry, frozen and replaceable configuration, flow detector thresholding and saturation.

  restored snapshot, and after \code{reseed}; stream independence from population changes; invariants

  \code{OrderBook}, \code{FundamentalValue}, and \code{MetaorderLog} to raise an error if any agent,
