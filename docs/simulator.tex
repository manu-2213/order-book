\begin{abstract}
This document describes the simulator used in the study \emph{Hiding in the Order Flow: Learning to
Execute Against Liquidity Providers that Detect Metaorders}. It is a deterministic, single-instrument,
price--time matching engine surrounded by a heterogeneous population of agents (zero-intelligence noise
flow, fundamentalists and chartists with Brock--Hommes switching, institutional traders executing labelled
metaorders in five styles, Avellaneda--Stoikov market makers with configurable detection responses and
volatility fragility, and an order-anticipation trader) in which detection of large orders emerges from
agents that see only public data. We document every rule, equation and default parameter, the
randomness model that makes runs exactly reproducible and counterfactuals paired, the validation
methodology against stylised facts and the square-root impact law, the measured results of the
validation, the automated tests, and the commands that reproduce everything. Part~II documents the
measurement phase that needs no reinforcement learning: the execution task, the baseline strategies
and their calibration, the held-out and learned detectors, detector scoring and the statistical
protocol, and the detectability (E2) and leakage (E3) experiments. Sections are written so that they
can be lifted into the paper's methods section and appendices.
\end{abstract}

%==================================================================================================
\section{Purpose and scope}
%==================================================================================================

The research question is how much of an execution's cost is caused by \emph{information leakage}
(other agents detecting the order and reacting) as opposed to mechanical market impact, and whether a
learning executor can reduce that leakage against detectors it has never seen. Answering it needs a
market in which (i) the reaction to a large order is produced by other agents rather than assumed by an
impact model, (ii) detection is both possible and genuinely hard because other institutions trade
large orders too, (iii) every agent, detector and executor sees only public information, and (iv) runs
are exactly reproducible so that the cost of leakage can be measured by counterfactual simulation with
common random numbers.

The \code{order\_book} package implements that market. This document covers the simulator and its
validation (Phases~0 and~1 of the research plan). The execution task, the reinforcement-learning
executor and the adversary ladder beyond levels A0, A1 and A4 are later phases and are only mentioned
where the simulator already provides for them.

\paragraph{Units.} Prices are integer \emph{ticks} (the default initial mid is $10\,000$ ticks, i.e.\
\$100.00 at a one-cent tick), quantities are integer \emph{lots}, cash is in ticks$\times$lots, and time
is in \emph{steps}. One step is a configurable unit of wall-clock time (\code{SimConfig.step\_seconds},
default one second); every rate in the configuration is per step.

%==================================================================================================
\section{Matching engine (\code{order\_book.book})}
%==================================================================================================

\code{OrderBook} is a deterministic single-instrument matching engine. It generates no liquidity and
assumes nothing about who trades or why; the caller serialises actions, and call order determines
arrival priority.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Priority.} Best price first, first-in-first-out within a price. Trades execute at the
  resting order's price.
\item \textbf{Order types.} A \emph{limit} order matches at its limit or better and rests for the
  remainder. A \emph{market} order consumes available liquidity and cancels any remainder
  (immediate-or-cancel). \code{Market.ioc} is a limit that cancels what would rest.
\item \textbf{Self-trade prevention.} Default \code{cancel\_newest}: the incoming remainder is cancelled
  when it would match the same client's resting order. \code{cancel\_oldest} removes the resting order
  and continues; \code{cancel\_both} removes both; \code{none} permits self-matching. Fills made before
  the encounter stand.
\item \textbf{Modification.} Reducing size retains priority. Increasing size or changing price replaces
  the order with a new identifier and priority. Invalid modifications leave the original intact.
\item \textbf{Ownership.} Cancellation and modification check the client id when it is given.
\item \textbf{Bounded window.} Each side admits prices within $N$ ticks (\code{num\_levels}, default 20) of
  an anchor: its own best price when populated, one tick inside the opposite best when it is empty, and
  the last trade price when both sides are empty. Orders outside the window are rejected; an
  improvement of the best price can \emph{evict} resting orders that fall outside the new window, and
  the report lists them so that the accountant can release reservations. Empty sides never manufacture
  quotes: \code{mid\_price} and \code{spread} are undefined unless both sides rest. This bounded book is
  not a normal exchange rule; it keeps observations finite and is part of the environment's dynamics.
\item \textbf{Reports.} Every operation returns a report naming the maker order of every fill, the orders
  removed by self-trade prevention and by eviction, and requested/filled/resting/cancelled quantities
  that always sum. Reports are shared read-only objects.
\item \textbf{Complexity.} Best quotes, mid, spread, per-level volume, per-client order lookup and single
  order lookup are $O(1)$.
\end{itemize}

%==================================================================================================
\section{Accounting and routing (\code{order\_book.market})}
\label{sec:market}
%==================================================================================================

\code{Market} owns every \code{Account} (cash, shares, reserved cash, reserved shares) and is the only
path from an agent to the book. Resting bids reserve $\text{price}\times\text{quantity}$ of cash and
resting asks reserve shares; reservations are running totals updated on placement, fill (both sides),
cancellation, self-trade cancellation and eviction, so that free balances are $O(1)$ and
\[
  \text{free cash} = \text{cash} - \text{reserved cash} \ge 0, \qquad
  \text{free shares} = \text{shares} - \text{reserved shares} \ge 0
\]
hold at all times. A limit order is capped at what the account can fund; a market buy walks the ask
levels and takes only what the free cash can pay for. Orders cut to zero are counted in
\code{budget\_rejects}; orders outside the window in \code{window\_rejects}. Every fill moves
$\text{price}\times\text{quantity}$ of cash from buyer to seller and the shares the other way, so
\[
  \sum_i \text{cash}_i \;\text{and}\; \sum_i \text{shares}_i \;\text{are conserved exactly}
\]
across the population (\code{Simulator.check\_conservation}). Nobody can short or borrow.

In analysis mode the market keeps a \emph{tape} of
$(\text{step}, \text{price}, \text{quantity}, \text{aggressor side}, \text{buyer}, \text{seller})$;
the buyer and seller identities are for offline analysis only and no agent reads the tape. The market
also hands the public state an \emph{anonymous} list of the step's aggressive fills,
$(\text{side}, \text{price}, \text{quantity})$, and the aggressive volume that enters the signed flow
window. \code{Market.hide(cid)} removes one client's aggressive trades from both (its trades still
happen, settle, move the price and count in the volume totals): this deliberately breaks the
no-identity rule and exists only for the analysis-mode counterfactual that separates impact cost from
leakage cost (Part~II, \S\ref{sec:e3}).

%==================================================================================================
\section{Public state (\code{order\_book.public\_state})}
\label{sec:public}
%==================================================================================================

\code{PublicState} is refreshed once per step from the book and the step's trades. It is the only
market information any participant may read. With $m_t$ the mid (the last trade price when a side is
empty), $\Delta m_t = m_t - m_{t-1}$, $V^{b}_t, V^{s}_t$ the step's buyer- and seller-initiated volume
and $V_t = V^b_t + V^s_t$, and EWMA weights $\alpha_f = 2/(\tau_f+1)$, $\alpha_s = 2/(\tau_s+1)$ with
spans $\tau_f = 10$ and $\tau_s = 100$:
\begin{align}
  \bar m^{f}_t &= \bar m^{f}_{t-1} + \alpha_f (m_t - \bar m^{f}_{t-1}), &
  \bar m^{s}_t &= \bar m^{s}_{t-1} + \alpha_s (m_t - \bar m^{s}_{t-1}), \\
  \sigma^{2,f}_t &= \sigma^{2,f}_{t-1} + \alpha_f (\Delta m_t^2 - \sigma^{2,f}_{t-1}), &
  \sigma^{2,s}_t &= \sigma^{2,s}_{t-1} + \alpha_s (\Delta m_t^2 - \sigma^{2,s}_{t-1}), \\
  \bar V^{f}_t &= \bar V^{f}_{t-1} + \alpha_f (V_t - \bar V^{f}_{t-1}), &
  \bar V^{s}_t &= \bar V^{s}_{t-1} + \alpha_s (V_t - \bar V^{s}_{t-1}).
\end{align}
Momentum indicators for three lookback pairs $(\ell_f,\ell_s) \in \{(5,30),(10,60),(20,120)\}$ are
differences of EMAs of the mid with those spans, $\mu^{(k)}_t = \text{EMA}_{\ell_f}(m)_t -
\text{EMA}_{\ell_s}(m)_t$. Signed aggressive flow over a rolling window of $W = 20$ steps gives the
imbalance
\begin{equation}
  I_t = \frac{\sum_{u=t-W+1}^{t} V^b_u - \sum_{u=t-W+1}^{t} V^s_u}{\sum_{u=t-W+1}^{t} V_u} \in [-1,1],
\end{equation}
maintained with a ring buffer rather than a tape scan. Finally an \emph{attention} multiplier, used by
the noise flow to cluster activity, is
\begin{equation}
  a_t = \min\!\Big(3,\ \max\!\Big(\tfrac12,\ 1 + \tfrac12\Big(\sqrt{\sigma^{2,f}_t/\sigma^{2,s}_t} - 1\Big)
        + \tfrac12\Big(\bar V^{f}_t/\bar V^{s}_t - 1\Big)\Big)\Big),
\end{equation}
held at 1 until the slow statistics have seen a full span (\code{warmed\_up}). The public state also
exposes best bid and ask, spread, last trade, and the step's volume. It contains no interpretation of
these statistics: anything that infers a metaorder from them is a \emph{detector} (\S\ref{sec:detectors}).

%==================================================================================================
\section{Randomness and reproducibility}
\label{sec:rng}
%==================================================================================================

A run is fully determined by a \code{SimConfig} and an integer seed. The simulator creates one root
\code{numpy.random.SeedSequence(seed)} and spawns from it, in a fixed order, one independent child
generator (PCG64) for
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item the \emph{scheduler}, which draws the random permutation in which background agents act each step;
\item the \emph{hidden value} process;
\item each background agent in construction order: market makers, the noise pool, institutional traders,
  the speculator population (one stream for the whole array-backed population), order anticipators;
\item any \emph{external} participant added later through the simulator's \code{new\_stream()} or
  \code{add\_external()} methods.
\end{enumerate}
Consequences:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Exact replay.} The same (config, seed) reproduces the event log byte for byte; so does
  restoring a snapshot and continuing. \code{reseed(seed')} re-derives every existing stream from a new
  root in place, so all holders follow it.
\item \textbf{Independence of population changes.} Removing the institutions or adding an anticipator
  (which is constructed last) leaves every earlier agent's stream untouched.
\item \textbf{Paired counterfactuals (common random numbers).} An external agent acts \emph{before} the
  background population and outside the scheduler's permutation, with its own stream. Running the same
  seed with and without it therefore shares the scheduler's and the hidden value's draws exactly, and
  every background agent's draws \emph{up to state dependence}: an agent that draws a state-dependent
  number of variates (the noise pool draws Poisson($\lambda a_t$) orders; speculators draw per active
  member) diverges only after the market itself has diverged because of the injected order. The
  difference between the two mid paths is then the injected order's impact and the market's reaction
  to it with the market's own randomness removed. This is the instrument used for the square-root
  impact measurement (\S\ref{sec:impact}) and, in Phase~2, for the leakage decomposition
  $\text{IS}_\text{visible} - \text{IS}_\text{invisible}$.
\item \textbf{Scope.} A Gymnasium episode reseeds a restored snapshot with a seed drawn from the
  environment's own generator, so parallel environments are independent and reproducible from the
  environment seed.
\end{itemize}

%==================================================================================================
\section{Agents}
\label{sec:agents}
%==================================================================================================

Every step, the hidden value advances, external agents act in fixed order, background agents act in
the scheduler's random order, the public state is refreshed, and the detectors are updated. Agents
read \code{Market.public}, the book's best quotes and levels, their own account and their own orders.
Cost scales with events, not with the number of notional traders.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Hidden value (\code{FundamentalValue})}
\label{sec:fundamental}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Exogenous information arrival is a random walk in ticks with optional reversion to its initial value,
\begin{equation}
  v_t = v_{t-1} + \rho\,(v_0 - v_{t-1}) + \sigma_v\,\varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \sim N(0,1),
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_v = 0.3$ and $\rho = 0$ by default. Only speculators see it, and only through private noise;
it drives beliefs, not prices. This is standard in agent-based models, and the paper's claim is limited
accordingly: microstructure and impact emerge from agents, the long-run price level does not.

\paragraph{Private valuations switch.} With \code{FundamentalConfig.private = True} there is no shared
value: each speculator $i$ holds a private valuation that drifts with its own noise and adapts toward
the traded mid,
\begin{equation}
  v_{i,t} = v_{i,t-1} + \kappa_a\,(m_{t-1} - v_{i,t-1}) + \sigma_v\,\varepsilon_{i,t},
\end{equation}
updated for all members once per step ($\kappa_a = 0.01$ by default). The shared path is still advanced
(so the stream structure is unchanged) but unused. The plan asks for the main results under both
information models.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Noise flow (\code{NoiseFlow})}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One pooled account submits zero-intelligence order flow. Each step it first cancels a random
subset of its resting orders, Binomial$(n_\text{resting}, p_c)$ of them with $p_c = 0.015$, then submits
$N_t \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda\, a_t)$ new orders ($\lambda = 2.5$, $a_t$ the attention multiplier).
Each order has size $U\{1,\dots,q_{\max}\}$ ($q_{\max}=12$) and is a buy with probability
\begin{equation}
  p_\text{bid} = \tfrac12 + w\,(c_t - \tfrac12), \qquad
  c_t = \frac{\text{cash}}{\text{cash} + \text{shares}\cdot m_t},
\end{equation}
($w = 0.5$), i.e.\ tilted toward the pool's cash share of wealth so it cannot exhaust its cash or its
stock in a long trend. A fraction $f_m = 0.15$ of orders are market orders at half size (takers are
smaller than makers), a fraction $f_x = 0.05$ are marketable limits at the opposite best, and the rest
rest $1 + \text{Geometric}$ ticks behind the opposite best with mean offset $\bar d + 1$ ($\bar d = 3$).

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Speculators (\code{Speculators})}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An array-backed population of $n = 40$ members, each either a \emph{fundamentalist} (mode 0) or a
\emph{chartist} (mode 1). Each step a vectorised draw selects the active members (each with probability
$p_a = 0.05$); only they do any work. An active member first cancels any resting order it has, then:

\paragraph{Fundamentalist.} Forms the belief $b_{i,t} = v_t + \beta_i + \eta_{i,t}$ with a permanent
private bias $\beta_i \sim N(0, 3^2)$ and fresh observation noise $\eta_{i,t} \sim N(0, 1^2)$ (ticks).
With gap $g = b_{i,t} - m_t$ and threshold $\theta = 2$ ticks it does nothing if $|g| < \theta$;
otherwise it trades toward its belief with size
\begin{equation}
  q = \min\big(q_{\max},\ \max(1, \operatorname{round}(|g| - \theta))\big), \qquad q_{\max} = 20,
\end{equation}
capped by its position limit (the share endowment by default). For $|g| \ge g_a = 8$ ticks it uses a
market order; otherwise a passive limit at $\lfloor b - \theta \rfloor$ (buy) or
$\lceil b + \theta \rceil$ (sell), kept at least one tick inside the opposite best.

\paragraph{Chartist.} Follows the public momentum indicator $\mu^{(k_i)}_t$ of its assigned lookback
pair. If it has a position and the signal has reversed ($\text{position}\cdot\mu < 0$) or faded
($|\mu| < \theta_c/4$), it exits by market order in chunks of at most $q_c = 10$ lots. Otherwise, if
$|\mu| \ge \theta_c$ it enters in the direction of $\mu$ with size
$\min(q_c, \max(1, \operatorname{round}(s\,(|\mu| - \theta_c))))$, $s = 2$ lots per tick, within the
position limit. The threshold $\theta_c = 6$ ticks is the main lever on return autocorrelation
(\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Brock--Hommes switching.} Every $T_s = 50$ steps, each mode's fitness $F_k$ is updated with
the mean realised wealth return (in percent) of its current members over the block,
$F_k \leftarrow F_k + \lambda_F (\bar r_k - F_k)$, $\lambda_F = 0.1$, and every member re-draws its mode
with
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{chartist}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\beta_s (F_1 - F_0)}}, \qquad \beta_s = 2 .
\end{equation}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Institutional traders (\code{InstitutionalTrader})}
\label{sec:institutions}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Institutional traders (four by default) are what makes detection hard: their metaorders look like an
executor's and create false positives for every detector. Each trader runs at most one metaorder at a
time.

\paragraph{Arrival.} While idle, a metaorder starts with probability $\lambda_I = 5\times 10^{-4}$ per
step (a discrete Poisson process; expected idle time 2\,000 steps).

\paragraph{Side.} With inventory relative to the initial endowment $x = (\text{shares} -
\text{shares}_0)/\text{shares}_0$, the metaorder is a buy with probability
\begin{equation}
  p_\text{bid} = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12 \tanh(\pi_I\, x), \qquad \pi_I = 2,
\end{equation}
so each trader's holding mean-reverts and the aggregate institutional flow stays balanced over long
runs. (Without this, the population's inventory random-walks and, over tens of thousands of steps,
exhausts the other agents' budgets; see \S\ref{sec:results}.)

\paragraph{Size and participation.} Size is log-normal, $Q = \min(Q_{\max}, \operatorname{round}(
Q_\text{med}\, e^{\sigma_Q Z}))$ with $Q_\text{med} = 600$ lots, $\sigma_Q = 0.7$, $Q_{\max} = 3\,000$,
capped by what the account can fund. The target participation rate is $\pi \sim U(0.02, 0.20)$ of
market volume. The implied duration uses the public slow volume EWMA $\bar V^s$ (a fallback of
5~lots/step before warm-up),
\begin{equation}
  T = \operatorname{clamp}\!\Big(\frac{Q}{\pi\, \bar V^s},\ 100,\ 4\,000\Big) \text{ steps}.
\end{equation}
With a market volume of about 5 lots/step, $Q$ ranges roughly from 150 to 2\,500 lots, which overlaps
the executor's $Q$ of 5--20\% of 2\,000 steps of volume (500--2\,000 lots).

\paragraph{Execution styles.} Each metaorder draws one of five styles (uniformly by default;
\code{style\_weights} can change this). Children are sent every $\Delta = 5$ steps. Progress is read
from the trader's own account (shares relative to the start), so passive fills need no callbacks. Let
$R$ be the remaining quantity, $S$ the number of slices left, $X$ the quantity executed so far.
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[\code{twap\_market}] every $\Delta$ steps, a market order of $\lceil R/S \rceil$.
\item[\code{twap\_random}] as above with the child size multiplied by $U(1-j, 1+j)$ and the next interval
  by $U(1-j,1+j)$, $j = 0.5$ (the ``naive'' way to hide; Yang \& Zhu predict randomisation).
\item[\code{twap\_passive}] each slice of $\lceil R/S \rceil$ is posted as a limit order at the trader's
  own best (best bid for a buy); at the slice deadline the resting order is cancelled and the unfilled
  part of the slice is crossed by market order (``catch-up''). This is what practitioners run.
\item[\code{pov}] every $\Delta$ steps, bring $X$ up to $\min(Q, \lfloor \pi \cdot (\text{public volume
  since the start}) \rfloor)$ by market order, where the trader itself accumulates the public per-step
  volume; over the final tenth of the horizon it switches to catch-up TWAP.
\item[\code{front\_loaded}] an Almgren--Chriss-like trajectory, remaining$(t) = Q\,\sinh(k(T-t))/\sinh(kT)$
  with $kT = 2$, brought up to schedule by market order every $\Delta$ steps.
\end{description}
Whatever remains at the planned end is forced through by market order, as an execution desk would do,
and its cost counts in full.

\paragraph{Labels.} In analysis mode every metaorder is recorded in \code{Simulator.metaorders.records}
as a \code{MetaorderRecord}: id, client, side, style, intended size, participation, start, planned end,
arrival mid, actual end, executed quantity and notional, from which the average price and the
implementation shortfall in basis points,
\begin{equation}
  \text{IS} = \sign \cdot \frac{\bar p - m_\text{arrival}}{m_\text{arrival}} \times 10^4,
  \qquad \sign = +1 \text{ for a buy}, -1 \text{ for a sell},
\end{equation}
follow. These are the ground truth for scoring detectors (ROC~AUC, detection delay, false alarms).
Agents only \emph{open} their own record; the guard test fails if any agent reads the list. In training
mode no labels are kept.

\paragraph{Injection.} \code{InstitutionalTrader.begin(side, size, participation, style, duration)}
starts a metaorder on demand. Together with \code{Simulator.add\_external} this is how the impact
experiment injects a single metaorder into an otherwise identical market.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Market makers (\code{MarketMaker})}
\label{sec:mm}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four two-sided post-only quoters in the spirit of Avellaneda--Stoikov. With inventory $I$ relative to the
initial holding, $\gamma = 0.003$, $\kappa = 1$, horizon $H = 50$ steps and
$\sigma^2 = \max(\sigma^{2,f}_t, \sigma^2_{\min})$ ($\sigma^2_{\min} = 0.25$ ticks$^2$/step):
\begin{align}
  r_t &= m_t - \operatorname{clamp}(I\,\gamma\sigma^2 H,\ -\bar s,\ \bar s), &
  h_t &= \operatorname{clamp}\!\Big(\tfrac12\big(\gamma\sigma^2 H + \tfrac{2}{\gamma}\ln(1+\tfrac{\gamma}{\kappa})\big),\ 1,\ \bar s\Big),
\end{align}
with $\bar s = 10$ ticks capping both the inventory skew and the half-spread so that a volatility spike
cannot make the quoter cross the book. Quotes are $\text{bid} = r_t - h_t - \omega^b$,
$\text{ask} = \max(r_t + h_t + \omega^a,\ \text{bid} + 2)$, then clamped to be post-only against
everyone else's best (\code{best\_excluding} the maker's own order). Quote size on each side is drawn
uniformly from $[5, 20]$ lots when a quote is (re)placed. A side is cancelled and replaced only when its
target price has moved at least one tick from the resting quote or its remaining size has fallen below
half of target, so the maker keeps queue position otherwise. The side that would push $|I|$ beyond
$I_{\max} = 200$ is pulled.

\paragraph{Detection response.} A maker whose configuration names a detector reads its signal
$s_t \in [-1,1]$ (positive: buy metaorder suspected). The pressured side is the ask for $s_t > 0$ and
the bid for $s_t < 0$. The response is
\begin{equation}
  r_t \mathrel{+}= \delta_r\, s_t, \qquad \omega^\text{pressured} = \delta_w\,|s_t|, \qquad
  \text{size}^\text{pressured} \mathrel{\times}= \max(0,\ 1 - \delta_q\,|s_t|),
\end{equation}
with $\delta_r = 4$ ticks, $\delta_w = 3$ ticks and $\delta_q = 0.7$. The mapping from signal to action
is the same for every adversary level; only the detector changes, so differences in execution cost
across levels come from detection quality alone. Running makers with and without detection side by side
makes false alarms costly: a maker that widens on a false alarm loses volume and spread income to the
others.

\paragraph{Fragility.} With the volatility ratio $\varrho_t = \sqrt{\sigma^{2,f}_t/\sigma^{2,s}_t}$,
both quote sizes are scaled by $\max(0,\ 1 - \phi\,\max(\varrho_t - 1, 0))$, $\phi = 0.5$, and when
$\varrho_t > \varrho_\text{pull} = 3$ both quotes are cancelled and stay away for a cooldown of 20
steps. Liquidity therefore thins exactly when a large order makes the price move fast, which is how
market impact becomes concave and how fast moves amplify.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Order anticipator (\code{OrderAnticipator})}
\label{sec:anticipator}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The order-anticipation trader (adversary level A4; none by default) reads a detector and trades on it
with market orders. With $|s_t| \ge \theta_A = 0.5$ it trades in the signal's direction
$\min\big(c,\ \operatorname{round}(\varphi\,(|s_t| - \theta_A)/(1-\theta_A))\big)$ lots per step
($\varphi = 2$ lots/step, chunk $c = 10$) within a position limit of $P_{\max} = 200$ lots; with
$|s_t| < \theta_\text{fade} = 0.2$ it unwinds $c$ lots per step; in between it holds. With
\code{contrarian\_first} it trades \emph{against} the detected flow (supplying liquidity, as van Kervel
\& Menkveld report for the start of institutional executions) until the signal has been confident for
\code{switch\_after} $= 50$ steps, then with it. A false alarm costs the anticipator the round trip, so
detection is not free for it either.

%==================================================================================================
\section{Detectors (\code{order\_book.detectors})}
\label{sec:detectors}
%==================================================================================================

A detector implements \code{update(public\_state)}, called once per step after the public state is
refreshed, and exposes a signed \code{signal} $\in [-1,1]$ and probabilities \code{p\_buy},
\code{p\_sell} $\in [0,1]$ that a metaorder is active on each side. Detectors read only
\code{PublicState}. The simulator keeps a registry \code{Simulator.detectors} by name; market makers and
anticipators whose configuration names a detector hold a reference to it. No detector ever receives a
flag saying an executor is active.

\paragraph{Flow detector (level A1).} The rule-based detector keeps an EWMA of the public flow imbalance
with span $\tau_d = 10$,
\begin{equation}
  M_t = M_{t-1} + \tfrac{2}{\tau_d+1}\,(I_t - M_{t-1}),
\end{equation}
and emits
\begin{equation}
  s_t = \sign(M_t)\cdot \min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{|M_t| - \theta_d}{1-\theta_d}\Big)^{+}, \qquad
  p^\text{buy}_t = s_t^{+},\quad p^\text{sell}_t = (-s_t)^{+},
\end{equation}
with threshold $\theta_d \in \{0.2, 0.3, 0.45\}$ for the \emph{lenient}, \emph{medium} and
\emph{strict} variants (\code{FLOW\_THRESHOLDS} in \code{presets}). Held-out statistical detectors (A2), a
learned classifier (A3) and best-response detectors (A5) plug in behind the same interface in later
phases.

\paragraph{Adversary presets.} \code{presets.adversary(level, threshold)} returns the configuration for
A0 (no maker reacts to any detector, no anticipator), A1 (two of four makers react to the flow detector)
and A4 (A1 plus one anticipator). Only the detector and the presence of the anticipator differ.

\section{Simulator loop, snapshots and external participants}
%==================================================================================================

\code{Simulator(seed, config, analysis)} builds the book, the market, the hidden value, the detector

cash, share and free-balance invariants.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}
%==================================================================================================

\subsection{Metrics}

\code{order\_book.validation} runs several seeds in analysis mode after a burn-in and computes, from
the mid path $m_t$, the spread, the tape and the metaorder labels:
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[Liquidity] median spread in ticks; share of steps with an empty side.
\item[Returns] autocorrelation of $k$-step returns $m_{t+k} - m_t$ ($k = 10$) at lags
  $1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50$ (lag 5 therefore spans 50 steps); the target applies from lag 5 on.
\item[Fat tails] kurtosis $\E[r^4]/\E[r^2]^2$ of returns at horizons $1, 5, 20, 100$ steps.
\item[Clustering] autocorrelation of $|r|$ ($k=10$) at lags $1, 5, 20, 50, 100, 200$.
\item[Flow memory] autocorrelation of trade signs ($+1$ buyer-initiated) at lags $1, 10, 100,
  1\,000$ trades.
\item[Budgets] budget rejects per trade and their balance across sides.
\item[Speed] background steps per second in analysis mode.
\end{description}

\subsection{Impact measurement}
\label{sec:impact}

The square-root law, peak impact $\propto (Q/V)^\delta$ with $\delta \approx 0.5$, is measured in two
ways, both normalising size by $V = \bar V \cdot T_\text{ref}$, the mean market volume per step times a
reference horizon of $T_\text{ref} = 2\,000$ steps (the executor's horizon), so that $Q/V$ is on the
same axis as the execution task and spans the size distribution. (Normalising by the volume over the
metaorder's own life pins $Q/V$ to about $\pi/(1+\pi)$ and makes the exponent unidentifiable.)

\paragraph{Natural metaorders.} For every labelled institutional metaorder the signed mid change from
the arrival mid is read at completion (``impact at completion''), at its maximum over the execution
(``peak''), and 500 steps after completion (``residual''). Sizes are binned by $Q/V$ into log-spaced
quantile bins, impact is averaged per bin (negatives included), and $\log(\text{impact})$ is regressed on
$\log(Q/V)$ over bins with a positive mean. Reversion is reported as pooled ratios,
$\sum \text{residual}/\sum\text{peak}$ and $\sum\text{end}/\sum\text{peak}$. These statistics are
\emph{noisy}: the metaorder's impact of a few ticks sits on a mid path whose standard deviation over
the metaorder's life is of the same order or larger, so the per-metaorder standard deviation of the
impact is 10--30 ticks and hundreds of metaorders per bin are needed. They are reported as
informational.

\paragraph{Paired injection (primary).} For each seed and each fraction $f \in \{0.01, 0.02, 0.05,
0.1, 0.2\}$, an external institutional trader is added (\S\ref{sec:rng}) and, after the burn-in,
injects one buy metaorder of $Q = f \cdot \bar V \cdot T_\text{ref}$ lots executed as TWAP market
orders over exactly $T_\text{ref}$ steps (participation $f$). The impact path is the difference between
this run's mid and the mid of the identical seed without the injected trader. Impact at completion,
peak and residual are read off the paired path, binned by $f$ and fitted as above. The pairing removes
the market's own randomness up to state dependence and typically reduces the per-run standard deviation
to a few ticks. This is the measurement behind the ``Square-root impact (paired)'' row of the
validation table and \code{scripts/impact.py}.

\paragraph{What the validation found and changed.} Two defects of the market were found only on runs
longer than 10\,000 steps and fixed before these numbers were taken:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Budget exhaustion.} With a random side choice, the institutional population's aggregate
  inventory random-walked; on one seed it net-sold 7\,500 lots over 30\,000 steps, the noise pool ran
  out of cash, the speculators sat at their position caps and the mid fell to 1\,954 against a hidden
  value of 9\,963 (kurtosis 120, 637 rejected buys). The inventory-reverting side choice
  (\S\ref{sec:institutions}) and larger endowments for the absorbing agents (noise pool 6\,000 shares,
  speculators 400) removed it: all seeds now track the hidden value and rejects are negligible.
\item \textbf{Measurement power.} The natural-metaorder impact statistics cannot resolve the exponent
  with a few hundred metaorders; the paired injection method was introduced for this reason.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Return autocorrelation sweep.} The one failing stylised fact is positive autocorrelation of
10-step returns: $ac(1) \approx 0.15$--$0.3$ and, on some seeds, $|ac| > 0.05$ at lags 5--10 (50--100
steps). Ablations show that it comes from slow price adjustment: without speculators the mid trends
strongly ($ac \approx 0.25$--$0.3$ at all lags) because the makers quote around a mid they themselves
set, so inventory skew feeds back on itself and only the fundamentalists anchor the price; chartists
amplify it. A sweep over the fundamental volatility $\sigma_v \in \{0.3, 0.6\}$, the chartist threshold
$\theta_c \in \{3, 6\}$ and the maker risk aversion $\gamma \in \{0.002, 0.006\}$ (5 seeds $\times$
20\,000 steps each) gave, for $\max |ac|$ over lags $\ge 5$: raising $\sigma_v$ makes trending worse
(0.19--0.53); $\theta_c = 6$ with $\gamma = 0.002$ gives 0.053 (mean 0.023), $\gamma = 0.006$ with
$\theta_c = 3$ gives 0.095, and both together 0.096; kurtosis and liquidity stay on target throughout.
A second sweep on held-out seeds 5--9 over $\theta_c \in \{6, 8\}$ and $\gamma \in \{0.002, 0.003\}$ gave
$\max|ac|$ of 0.445 (one trending seed; mean 0.044), 0.053 (mean 0.016), 0.070 and 0.078 respectively.
The defaults were set to $\theta_c = 6$, $\gamma = 0.003$, and the reference run of
Table~\ref{tab:targets} (seeds 0--4, which were not used to choose between these two values) passes
every target. The remaining positive autocorrelation at lags 1--2 (10--20 steps; $ac(1) \approx 0.17$)
is allowed by the target and reflects the gradual price adjustment to metaorders that the study is
about.

\paragraph{Alternative presets.} The same validation was run on 5 seeds $\times$ 10\,000 steps for the
alternative configurations (these are shorter runs and the impact exponent on $n = 25$ paired runs is
correspondingly noisier):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Private valuations} (\code{--preset private}, $\kappa_a = 0.01$): liquidity, fat tails
  (kurtosis 103 $\to$ 5) and order-flow memory pass, but impact is nearly permanent (90\% of the peak
  at completion, 95\% after 500 steps) with $\delta \approx 0.9$, because every valuation adapts toward
  the new price and nothing anchors it. Slower adaptation ($\kappa_a = 10^{-3}$, $2\times10^{-4}$)
  restores reversion but then speculators drift to their position caps and budgets fail. The private
  information model is implemented and runs, but it needs its own calibration (likely a larger
  speculator endowment and a reversion term) before the main results are reported under it; this is
  an open item.
\item \textbf{A4} (one anticipator): with the original anticipator defaults (4 lots/step, limit 300) the
  anticipator was about 40\% of market volume when active and created a self-reinforcing loop (its own
  trades raised the imbalance it reacted to), trending the market ($\max|ac| = 0.54$) and making impact
  superlinear ($\delta = 1.24$ at completion). With the adopted defaults (2 lots/step, limit 200) the
  market stays regular ($\max|ac| = 0.08$, kurtosis 15 $\to$ 5) while impact is still amplified
  ($\delta = 0.79$ at completion, 0.66 at peak; 26\% of peak remains after 500 steps), which is the
  effect the adversary ladder is meant to produce. A4 is not required to pass the background-market
  validation (E1 is run on the background market only), but it must not destabilise it.
\end{itemize}


\part{Measurement without reinforcement learning}
\label{part:two}

This part implements Phase~2 of the plan: everything needed for experiments E2 and E3 and for the
Phase~2 gate (TWAP's leakage cost significantly above zero at one size or more). Nothing here learns
by reinforcement; the executor runs fixed strategies, and the only trained component is the A3
classifier, which never sees an executor.

%==================================================================================================
\section{The execution task and the executor agent (\code{order\_book.execution.task})}
\label{sec:task}
%==================================================================================================

\paragraph{Task.} An \code{ExecutionTask} fixes the side (buy or sell), the size as a fraction $f$ of
expected market volume over the horizon, $Q = \operatorname{round}(f\,\bar V\,T)$ with
$f \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, the horizon $T = 2\,000$ steps, the decision interval $N = 5$ steps, and
the hard limits: at most two resting orders, no marketable limit more than 5 ticks through the
opposite best, and an optional cap on the market-order size per decision as a share of expected
interval volume (unused by the baselines, so that \emph{Immediate} can exist; the RL environment will
set it). $\bar V$ is the mean traded volume per step over the burn-in, read from the public tape, so
it is identical for every strategy and adversary level on the same seed.

\paragraph{Executor.} \code{ExecutorAgent} is an external participant (\S\ref{sec:rng}): it acts first
in every step with its own stream and outside the scheduler's permutation. It starts at a given step
(the end of the burn-in), records the arrival mid, and every $N$ steps hands its strategy an
\code{ExecState} and applies the returned \code{Decision} (optionally cancel resting orders; a market
order; then a limit order at a price, default own best). Fills are read off the executor's own
account (shares and cash relative to the start), so passive fills need no callbacks, and each fill is
tagged aggressive or passive. At $T$ the agent cancels what rests and sends the remainder by market
order, repeating for up to 50 steps if the book is thin; the forced quantity is reported and its cost
counts in full. The result records executed and forced quantity, notional, aggressive share, orders
sent and the fill list, and
\begin{equation}
  \text{IS} = \sign\cdot\frac{\bar p - m_\text{arrival}}{m_\text{arrival}} \times 10^4 \ \text{bps},
  \qquad \text{completion} = \frac{Q_\text{executed} - Q_\text{forced}}{Q}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{What a strategy sees.} \code{ExecState}: elapsed and remaining time, decisions left, $Q$,
executed and remaining quantity, aggressive quantity, arrival mid, mid, best quotes, spread, the public
flow imbalance, public volume since the start, the executor's own resting orders, the expected volume
per step, the executor's own share of the aggressive volume in the public flow window (its
\emph{footprint}), and the \code{PublicState} itself. It never sees any detector's output or any
other agent's state.

%==================================================================================================
\section{Baseline strategies (\code{order\_book.execution.strategies})}
\label{sec:baselines}
%==================================================================================================

Let $R$ be the remaining quantity, $S$ the decisions left, $X$ the executed quantity, $t$ the elapsed
steps. All sizes are rounded up to whole lots.

\paragraph{Classical schedules.}
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[\code{immediate}] market order for $Q$ at the first decision.
\item[\code{twap\_market}] market order for $\lceil R/S \rceil$ every decision.
\item[\code{twap\_passive}] each decision: cancel, cross by market order the unfilled part of the
  previous slice (catch-up), then post the new slice $\lceil (R - \text{catch-up})/S \rceil$ at own best.
\item[\code{twap\_random}] slice $\lceil R/S \rceil \cdot U(0.5, 1.5)$; with probability 0.3 a
  decision is skipped (timing jitter) unless it is the last.
\item[\code{pov}] every decision bring $X$ up to $\min(Q, \lfloor 0.10 \cdot \text{public volume since
  the start}\rfloor)$; TWAP catch-up over the final tenth of the horizon.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Model-based schedules} (parameters from the \code{Calibration}, \S\ref{sec:calibration}).
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[\code{almgren\_chriss\_$\lambda$}] target trajectory $X(t) = Q\,[1 - \sinh(\kappa(T-t))/\sinh(\kappa T)]$,
  $\kappa = \sqrt{\lambda\sigma^2/\eta}$, for $\lambda \in \{10^{-7}, 10^{-6}, 10^{-5}\}$ ($\kappa T
  \approx 0.6, 2, 6$ with the default calibration), executed with the passive-with-catch-up tactic:
  each decision, cross the shortfall against $X(t)$ by market order and post the increment to
  $X(t+N)$ at own best.
\item[\code{obizhaeva\_wang}] with resilience $\rho$: a block $x_0 = Q/(\rho T + 2)$ at the start and at
  the end and a constant rate $\rho x_0$ in between, all by market order.
\item[\code{limit\_market}] a limit-and-market policy in the spirit of Gu\'eant--Lehalle--Fernandez-Tapia
  and Cartea--Jaimungal: keep a resting order of two slices at the deepest distance $d$ whose fitted
  per-decision fill probability $p_d$ keeps pace with the linear schedule, $p_d \ge \tfrac12 (1 - u\,
  \tau)$ with $\tau$ the fraction of time remaining and $u = 0.5$ (quote deeper early, tighten toward
  the end); keep the order while its price is still the chosen one (queue position); let execution lag
  the schedule by up to $1/p_d$ slices before market orders catch up; market orders over the last two
  decisions.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Stealth heuristics.}
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[\code{footprint}] base slice $R/S$ scaled by $U(0.6, 1.4)$; $\times 0.25$ when the executor's own
  share of the public signed flow exceeds 0.3 (too visible); $\times 1.5$ when the public imbalance
  exceeds 0.2 in the executor's direction (cover in the natural flow); half of each slice posted
  passively; TWAP catch-up with market orders over the last 15\% of the horizon.
\item[\code{mimic}] draws one of the five institutional execution styles per episode (with the
  institutional jitter, front-loading and a POV rate $U(0.05, 0.20)$) and runs it, so the executor is
  statistically one more institution.
\end{description}

%==================================================================================================
\section{Calibration (\code{order\_book.execution.calibration})}
\label{sec:calibration}
%==================================================================================================

The model-based strategies' parameters are fitted under A0 on training seeds by
\code{scripts/calibrate.py}; the defaults in \code{Calibration} are the values measured on the default
market so that every strategy runs without a calibration file.
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[$\sigma$] standard deviation of one-step mid changes on the background market (default 0.19 ticks).
\item[$\eta$ (temporary impact)] slope of the mean child slippage---fill price minus the mid of the
  previous step, in the order's direction---on the trading rate $Q/T$, across TWAP-market runs at
  $f \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.2\}$ (default 0.035 ticks per lot/step; the intercept, about 0.55 ticks, is the
  half-spread).
\item[permanent impact] paired impact at completion per lot (default 0.0017 ticks/lot).
\item[$\rho$ (resilience)] exponential rate fitted by log-linear regression to the paired impact path
  after completion, relative to its final level (default 0.0028 per step).
\item[depth] mean resting lots within 5 ticks of the best on one side (default 227).
\item[fill probabilities] a \code{FillProber} agent posts 1-lot limits at 0, 1, 2 and 3 ticks behind its
  own best every $N$ steps on alternating sides and cancels them at the next decision; the fill share
  per distance is $p_d$ (default $(0.28, 0.015, 0.001, 0)$: in a one-tick-spread book only the best
  is ever reached within 5 steps, which is why passive tactics mostly end up catching up).
\end{description}

%==================================================================================================
\section{Held-out and learned detectors}
\label{sec:a2a3}
%==================================================================================================

All detectors implement the interface of \S\ref{sec:detectors}. The A2 and A3 detectors share a
\code{FeatureTracker} that maintains, from the anonymous per-step trade list and the public
aggregates: the flow imbalance over 20, 50 and 200 steps; the imbalance over the last 100 lots
(volume clock); the signed length of the current run of same-sign aggressive trades; the
\emph{regularity} of that run, $1 - \text{CV}$ of its child sizes; the ratio of the trade rate over
20 steps to that over 200; the 50-step return in units of the fast volatility; the spread; and the
trade-count imbalance over 20 steps.

\paragraph{A2 (held-out, never used in training).}
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[\code{runs}] $s = \sign(\text{run}) \cdot \operatorname{clip}((\text{run length} - 4)/6, 0, 1)$.
\item[\code{cusum}] two-sided CUSUM with restart on the imbalance $x$ of consecutive 20-step blocks:
  $S^+ \leftarrow \max(0, S^+ + x - k)$, $S^- \leftarrow \max(0, S^- - x - k)$, $k = 0.1$; when either
  exceeds $h = 1.5$ an alarm is raised in that direction, both statistics restart, and the alarm is
  held as a signal decaying linearly to zero over 10 blocks; between alarms $s = (S^+ - S^-)/h$. (A
  CUSUM without restart stays in alarm because institutions keep the flow one-sided most of the time.)
\item[\code{volume\_clock}] the 100-lot imbalance thresholded like the flow detector ($\theta = 0.3$).
\item[\code{regularity}] metaorder reconstruction in miniature (Goliath \& Gebbie, 2026): for a
  same-sign run of at least 3 trades, $s = \sign \cdot \text{regularity} \cdot \min(1, \text{run}/6)$.
\end{description}

\paragraph{A3 (learned).} A multinomial logistic classifier on the standardised features with classes
\{none, buy metaorder active, sell metaorder active\} (net side when several overlap), trained with
Adam on the class-weighted cross-entropy by \code{scripts/train\_detector.py} on background runs of
the A1-medium market (training seeds 100--139, validation seeds 500--507 by default), with labels from
the institutional metaorders only. The signal is $p_\text{buy} - p_\text{sell}$. Training prints one
line per epoch (loss, validation ROC AUC for the buy class, the sell class and the unsigned signal) and
the fitted weights. The classifier never sees an executor, so its score on the executor measures how
institutional the executor looks.

%==================================================================================================
\section{Scoring and statistical protocol (\code{order\_book.scoring})}
\label{sec:scoring}
%==================================================================================================

\paragraph{Detectors.} ROC AUC is the rank-based Mann--Whitney statistic with average ranks for ties.
In E2 the positives are the steps on which the executor is active and the negatives all other steps
of the window (institutional-only and quiet steps alike), so institutional false positives count
against the detector exactly as they would in the market. The \emph{signed} AUC uses the signal in
the executor's direction. Detection delay is the number of steps from the executor's start to the
first signal at or above the operating threshold (0.5) in its direction (the full window when never
reached); median and 90th percentile are reported. False-alarm rates are the share of steps with
$|s| \ge 0.5$ among steps where only institutions are active and among quiet steps. The same
detector's AUC on the institutional metaorders of the episode is reported for comparison.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Every comparison is paired: all strategies and adversary levels run on the
same seeds, the executor's side is fixed by the seed's parity, and the burn-in state is identical.
Means come with 95\% percentile-bootstrap intervals over seeds (2\,000 resamples); paired differences
with a bootstrap interval and a one-sided bootstrap $p$-value for ``$> 0$''; Holm's step-down
correction is applied over the family of leakage tests; tail risk is CVaR at 95\%; results are also
reported by side. The per-episode shortfall is dominated by the market's own drift over the horizon
(standard deviation of order 10~bps on a 2\,000-step horizon), which the pairing removes: in a
four-seed pilot the paired leakage interval was already $\pm 0.4$~bps.

\subsection{E2: detectability of classic strategies}

\subsection{E3: leakage cost}
\label{sec:e3}

\code{E3Config}: all twelve baselines $\times$ sizes \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\} $\times$ adversaries \{A0,
A1-medium, A4\} $\times$ \{visible, invisible\} $\times$ 20 test seeds, plus one run per seed and
adversary with no executor (the market's own path). ``Invisible'' means \code{Market.hide} is applied
to the executor: detectors and the public flow statistics ignore its trades while everything else is
unchanged, so on the same seed
\begin{equation}
  \text{IS}_\text{visible} = \underbrace{\text{IS}_\text{invisible}}_{\text{impact cost}}
  + \underbrace{\text{IS}_\text{visible} - \text{IS}_\text{invisible}}_{\text{leakage cost}} .
\end{equation}
Under A0 the two runs are identical to the last digit (nobody reads the detector), which the tests
assert; under A1 and A4 the difference is caused by detection alone up to the state dependence of
\S\ref{sec:rng}. Per strategy, size and adversary the summary reports $n$, mean IS visible and
invisible with intervals, the paired leakage with interval, $p$-value and Holm-adjusted $p$-value,
CVaR$_{95}$, completion, forced share and results by side. With \code{--paths} the mid paths are
saved and the impact path (executor minus no-executor run) is written to
\code{outputs/e3/impact\_paths.npz}. The script ends with the Phase~2 gate verdict: TWAP (market)
leakage $> 0$ with $p < 0.05$ at one size or more under A1 or A4.

\paragraph{Pilot.} A four-seed pilot of TWAP (market) at the full horizon gave a paired leakage of
$+0.37$~bps [0.08, 0.83] at $f = 0.10$ under A1-medium ($p < 0.001$), $-0.25$ under A4 at the same
size, and $-0.01$ / $+0.17$ at $f = 0.20$: the gate passed in the pilot, but the effect is small
(tenths of a basis point against an impact cost of a few basis points) because the flow detector is
rarely confident at these participation rates ($|s| > 0.5$ on 1--7\% of steps). The full E3 run
decides; per the plan, if leakage is not significant the result is reported before detector strength
or cover is recalibrated.
